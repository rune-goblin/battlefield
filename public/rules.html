Start here

Each army is one unit. Destroy enemy units or break their Morale to win the field.

3actions per activation
4maximum Health
3maximum Morale

Your turn

  1. Choose a unit. Each unit acts once per round. The side with more units still to act goes next. On a tie, sides alternate; the attacker wins the first tie.
  2. Spend up to three actions. Move, attack, Guard or Rally. Each activity lists its cost. A unit may make one attack per activation, including a siege attack or Blast spell.
  3. End the activation. Choose the next side using the same rule. After all units act, start a new round.

Your basic actions

ActivityCostEffect
Move1Move up to your Speed in hexes on open ground. Difficult terrain costs more. Stop when you enter an enemy’s zone of control; leaving one takes a Reflex roll.
Step1Move one hex onto open ground without a roll, even out of an enemy’s zone of control.
Strike1Attack an adjacent enemy in melee. Roll d20 + Melee against its Defence.
Fire1Shoot a visible enemy within your weapon’s range. Roll d20 + Shoot against its Defence. Your unit must be free of enemy contact.
Brace1Gain +2 Defence until your next activation.
Steady1Roll d20 + Will against your Rally DC. Success restores 1 Morale; critical success restores 2. At full Morale, success inspires you: +2 to your next roll. Critical failure costs 1 Morale.

Example: Move one hex, Strike an adjacent enemy, then Brace. That spends all three actions.

Attack and damage

Meet the target’s Defence to hit for 1 damage. Beat it by 10 to score a critical hit for 2 damage. A natural 20 improves the result one step; a natural 1 worsens it one step. Damage removes Health. At 0 Health, the unit is destroyed.

After taking damage, the target rolls d20 + Fortitude against the attacker’s level DC (difficulty class). Failure costs 1 Morale. If a melee attack misses, the attacker rolls d20 + Will against the target’s level DC or loses 1 Morale. A critical miss in melee also gives the attacker −2 Defence until its next activation.

Morale and contact

Each missing Morale point gives −1 to rolls and Defence. At 0 Morale, the unit routs: it must retreat toward its home edge. An ally can restore its Morale before it leaves.

Adjacent enemies are engaged when they can attack across their shared edge. Each enemy controls the hexes around it. Leaving its zone of control with a Move takes a Reflex roll; a Step to open ground takes none. Two or more enemies in contact outflank a unit, giving it −2 Defence.

Victory

At round end, a side loses the field if it has no standing units. A standing unit is on the field, alive and above 0 Morale. Otherwise, dusk ends the day after six rounds by default.

Setup

  1. Generate the battlefield. Choose terrain, a river or lake if needed, and fortifications. Generate a map and reroll until it suits the battle.
  2. Edit the map. Paint terrain, height, walls and gates. Height ranges from −2 to 2. Add bridges or shallows where ground troops need to cross water.
  3. Place siege engines. Use dry hexes. Engines start loaded unless you choose otherwise.
  4. Build both armies. Choose troops from the library, generate a force, or create custom cards. Set current Health and Morale.
  5. Deploy the defender, then the attacker. On the default board, attackers use ranks 1–4 and defenders use ranks 12–15. Ambush allows one extra rank toward the centre. Use the marked home zones on smaller boards.
  6. Begin. Each side needs at least one unit. Every unit needs a legal hex. The side with more units acts first; the attacker acts first on a tie.

A unit on an engine claims it. Otherwise, an adjacent unit can claim it for its side. Units must occupy an engine’s hex to operate it. A unit may start hauling a mobile engine. Engines outside the board take no part.

Shared command

Give each player a unit. If there are fewer armies than players, assign allied units or split an army before deployment. Each part uses the same card and has its own hex, Health and Morale. Each counts as a full unit for combat, outflanking and rout. After battle, rejoin the parts: average their Health and round down; use their lowest Morale.

The force generator suggests an opposing army with a similar unit count and total level. Adjust it to suit the battle. Place siege engines separately.

Rounds and turns

Each round represents one hour. Each unit activates once per round and has three actions. Haste grants an extra action.

  1. The side with more units still to act goes next.
  2. On a tie, the side that did not act last goes next. The attacker wins the first tie.
  3. That side chooses one of its units that has yet to act.
  4. Spend its actions, then end its activation.

One attack per activation: Melee, Charge, Shoot, Blast and siege attacks share this limit. Spend other actions on movement, Guard, Rally or other spells.

Round end

  1. Resolve ownership of empty engines. A side claims an engine if only its units stand beside it.
  2. Check victory. If one side has no standing units, the battle ends.
  3. At the daily limit, dusk ends the day. The default is six rounds; setup also offers eight.
  4. Otherwise, start the next round. Each engine regains its attack. Loading progress carries over.

Commitment

Spend one extra action for +2, or two for +4 on Melee, Charge, Shoot, Rally, Blast or Healing. Controlling gains the bonus to its spell DC. Spend only available actions; a spell’s total cost cannot exceed three actions.

The bonus applies to that activity’s roll, including all targets of a shared roll. It stacks with other bonuses. It changes neither damage nor targets, and it affects neither saves nor Aegis checks.

Example: spend all three actions on Strike at +4, or spend two on Strike at +2 and one on Brace.

Move, Step, Guard and the Offense, Defense and Movement spells use their listed costs without extra commitment. You may repeat non-attack activities, but cast from each spell tree only once per activation.

Movement

Move

Each Move action adds your Speed to your movement budget. Keep unused movement for later in the same activation.

DestinationMovement cost

Swamp3
One level uphillAt least 2

Use the highest cost from terrain, climbing or a siege field. A climb into forest costs 2; a climb into swamp costs 3. For example, a Speed-1 unit spends two actions to enter forest.

A Move stops when it enters an enemy’s zone of control, even the zone of an enemy you started beside. Walls and cliffs prevent contact across their edges. Use each army’s source land, fly and swim speeds. Each step uses the legal mode that spends the least movement. Flight crosses walls, cliffs and water at its own rate. Swimming crosses water and shallows at its own rate; walls and cliffs still block it. Hauling uses land Speed and the engine’s limit.

Movement controls

Drag a unit to preview its route. Press Space to add a waypoint under the pointer; press again there to remove it. Waypoints apply to Move and Charge. Only the final waypoint may enter a zone of control. Drop a unit on an adjacent open hex to Step. Step and Flee ignore waypoints.

Zone of control

Each enemy with a melee attack controls the hexes around it, except across a wall or cliff. A shooter that pins you also controls your hex. Each enemy whose zone you stand in holds you. A melee holder’s DC is its Battle DC; a pinning shooter’s is its Salvo DC.

Entering an enemy’s zone ends a Move. A Move that starts in one or more zones begins with one roll: d20 + Reflex − missing Morale, compared with each holder’s DC.

ResultEffect
You meet every holder’s DCMove normally.
You fall short of any holder’s DCStay where you are. The Move costs one action; you may try again with another.
You critically fail against a holderYou also stay, and that holder makes a free attack against your Defence: a Melee attack, or a Shoot attack from a pinning shooter.

A Move that stays beside a holder, sliding along its line, rolls in the same way and stops after one hex. A Step leaves a zone without a roll. Changing hex ends the pin.

No Retreat grants Hold Ground: the unit ignores its first forced displacement each round. Pursuit belongs to the separate reaction system.

Step

For one action, move one hex without a roll, into or out of any zone of control. The hex must cost 1 movement by land: open ground, a settlement, a bridge, or level or downhill ground. You cannot Step into forest, rough ground, shallows, swamp, an uphill hex or a siege field, and you cannot Step by flying or swimming. A rooted or pinned unit, or one with zero Speed, cannot Step. A routed unit Steps only toward its home edge.

Step is the sure way out of a zone of control on open ground. It covers one hex where a Move covers your Speed. In rough ground, a Move and its roll are the only way out.

Charge

A Charge moves up to twice your Speed and ends in a melee attack. It uses your attack for the activation. If the charge ends at least 3 hexes from where it began, the run-up adds +2 to the attack.

ActivityActionsAttack after moving
Charge2Strike
Charge and Press3Press
  • Start outside every enemy’s zone of control, and unpinned. End in contact with your target.
  • Enter no zone of control but the target’s along the route. Walls and cliffs block engagement across their edges.
  • Use open ground or bridges. Forest, rough ground, settlements, swamp, shallows and uphill steps stop a charge, including a flying unit’s charge. Sure footing allows these terrain crossings and climbs.
  • A charge from above gives the target −2 on its save against Morale loss.
  • Cavalry and units with Cavalry Charge charge with impact: a Charge lands as a Press and a Charge and Press as an Overrun, at the same action cost. An assignment can limit impact to the first Charge each battle.
  • Commitment adds to the run-up bonus; a Charge with one extra action attacks at up to +4.
  • Take cover prevents an Overrun from pushing its target. Unused charge movement expires.

By default, a charge ends at the nearest legal hex beside its target. Use a waypoint to choose another approach.

Flee

Leave through an outer edge in your deployment zone, including its side edges and any ambush rank. Pay the movement cost to reach the edge, then one action to Flee. Clear roots and pinning first. Move or Step out of every zone of control to reach the edge; a unit already on the edge may Flee directly, without the Reflex roll.

Roll Will with current modifiers against your Rally DC at the exit hex. You leave on every result.

ResultOutcome
Success or critical successKeep Health and Morale. Enter camp, take no more turns that day, and remain eligible for recovery and the next day.
Failure or critical failureMorale falls to 0. Leave routed, abandon equipment, and miss recovery and the next day.

A unit that already routs remains routed when it leaves. Units in camp survive but do not hold the field.

Combat and Health

Each unit has up to 4 Health. Damage removes Health. At 0, the unit is destroyed. A surviving unit keeps its normal statistics regardless of Health; Morale applies separate penalties.

Attacks

Roll d20 + Melee to fight in melee, d20 + Shoot to shoot, or d20 + spell attack to Blast. Apply bonuses and penalties, then compare with the target’s Defence. See Checks for critical results.

Attack resultTargetAttacker in melee
Critical successLose 2 Health; make a Fortitude save.No effect
SuccessLose 1 Health; make a Fortitude save.No effect
FailureNo effectMake a Will save.
Critical failureNo effectMake a Will save and become exposed: −2 Defence until your next activation.

Each save uses d20 + the save − missing Morale, with other modifiers. Roll against the opposing unit’s level DC. Failure costs 1 Morale. Fortitude resists Morale loss from damage; Will resists repulse after a missed melee attack.

Damage uses fixed values. The target responds during its own activation. A missed shot or Blast causes no repulse.

Melee

Attack an adjacent enemy across a passable edge.

ActivityActionsEffect
Strike1Make one attack.
Press2Strike. On a hit, the target rolls its Fortitude save twice and keeps the worse result. Failure costs 1 Morale.
Overrun3Press. On a hit, push the target one hex directly away and enter its former hex. If the attack destroys it, enter that hex.

An occupied hex or the board edge prevents the push without extra Morale loss. Water, a wall or a cliff corners the target: it stays in place and loses 1 extra Morale. Take cover prevents the push and cornering loss. The Press save still applies.

Guard

Guard lasts until your next activation. In swamp or shallows, only Brace is available.

Defence and modifiers

Start with the unit’s Defence. Add the highest circumstance bonus from Guard, terrain or wall cover. Subtract the worst circumstance penalty from swamp (−1), being outflanked (−2) or being exposed (−2). These circumstance bonuses and penalties do not stack within each group.

A unit is outflanked while engaged with at least two enemies. Subtract missing Morale and apply other conditions separately.

Rally

Any unit with at least 1 Morale may Rally, including while engaged. Roll d20 + Will − missing Morale, with other modifiers.

The Rally DC (also called rout DC) is the level DC of the highest-level enemy within 3 hexes of the recipient. If none is that close, use the highest-level enemy on the field.

ActivityActionsRecipients
Steady1Your unit
Rally2Your unit and one adjacent ally
Inspire3Your unit and all allies within 3 hexes

Roll once. Compare the result with each recipient’s Rally DC.

Critical successRestore 2 Morale. If this reaches 3, also become inspired.
SuccessRestore 1 Morale. If already at 3, become inspired instead.
FailureNo recovery.
Critical failureNo recovery. The unit that made the Rally loses 1 Morale.

Inspired: +2 to your next roll of any kind, including a save. It ends after that roll or at the end of your activation, whichever comes first. It requires full Morale and does not stack with another status bonus.

Extra commitment improves the shared roll. Three actions buy Steady at +4, Rally at +2, or Inspire at its normal modifier. You may also make separate Steady attempts.

Battlefield

The default board has 169 hexes. Columns run from a to o; ranks run from 1 to 15. The attacker starts at the low ranks, the defender at the high ranks. The centre is h8. Setup also offers the 91-hex and 61-hex boards, where each side deploys on three ranks.

Each hex holds one unit. All six neighbouring hexes are adjacent. Distance is the shortest number of hex steps between two positions.

A weapon reaches the end of its band and one hex beyond at −2. Shooting downhill extends range. Spells use fixed limits. See Shooting and Magic.

Game materials

  • A battle board and a troop sheet or unit card for each army.
  • A twenty-sided die (d20). The app handles rolls and modifiers.
  • Players to command the armies. The game master (GM) controls enemy factions and assigns allied units.

Campaign battles

A battle starts when hostile armies share a campaign hex. The army that entered is the attacker; the army already there is the defender. Use that hex’s terrain. Resolve the battle during the kingdom’s Actions phase, before its next action.

Sight

Trace a straight line between hex centres. An intervening hex blocks sight if it is at least as high as the higher unit and higher than the lower unit. Units on the same level can see across other hexes at that level.

A forest hex on the line gives the target +1 Defence against shooting and Blast. Two forest hexes block sight. Ignore the attacker’s own forest hex. Settlements grant the same cover but do not block sight. Forest cover does not improve Will saves.

Lines along an edge or through a corner use one side of that boundary. Each hex in a Blast area requires sight from the caster. Flight leaves sight rules unchanged. Walls allow shots across them and use the cover rules below.

Walls and fortifications

An intact, closed wall blocks ground movement and contact. Flying units can cross it. Ranged attacks can cross it.

A unit inside a wall enclosure is Fortified. An incoming ranged attack that crosses an intact, closed perimeter wall grants the target that wall’s cover bonus. An open wall provides local cover to its adjacent interior hex but grants no Fortified condition. Use only the highest applicable cover bonus.

A defender beside the inside of an intact, closed wall is garrisoned: +1 to troop shooting outward across that wall. Either army may use interior cover, but only the defending army gains the garrison bonus.

Entering or leaving an enclosure adds or removes Fortified. An open gate or breach leaves the condition in place, but attacks through that gap gain no wall cover. Attackers inside the courtyard bypass its outer walls. A separate inner wall can still provide cover.

High-angle siege attacks ignore cover. Hardness reduces damage to structures, not to units.

Attacking walls

Attack an adjacent wall segment in melee, or use a siege engine’s wall attack within range. Roll against 10 + wall tier + highest active defender level.

A normal melee attack deals 1 structural damage on a hit or 2 on a critical hit. Siege attacks list their damage and penetration. Subtract max(0, hardness − penetration) from damage, minimum 0.

At 0 structural HP, the segment becomes a permanent breach. Repair walls through campaign maintenance.

Gates

Open gate and Close gate each cost one action. Either side may operate a gate. The unit must stand at its interior hex, remain standing and be free of enemy contact.

An open gate allows movement and contact and grants no wall cover. Terrain, occupied hexes and cliffs still apply. A closed gate restores the barrier and cover. A breached gate stays open.

Map editing

Paint terrain and height separately. A two-level difference creates a cliff. A ground route runs from the attacker’s deployment zone to the defender’s without entering water or crossing a cliff or an intact wall; an open gate lets it through. A generated map always keeps one unless water cuts every route. If a river blocks every ground route, add bridges or shallows, or keep it as a deliberate obstacle.

Paint a gate on an existing wall. The Gate brush cycles through open and closed states on each facing, then removes the gate. Ring handles mark the interior face. The chosen facing and state carry into battle.

A closed wall loop defines an enclosure. Deleting a segment during setup breaks it. Generated forts at the map edge retain their courtyard; other open outlines grant local cover only.

A caster uses its spell attack for Blast and Healing. Enemies save Will against its spell DC for Controlling and Aegis. Apply the ordinary penalties from missing Morale and conditions.

Casting

  • Every unit takes three actions on its activation. Spell costs remain one, two, or three actions. Tier IV spells cost three actions.
  • Each caster may cast once from each tree per activation. Blast uses the same single attack allowance as Melee and Shoot. Casting uses actions and positioning; it requires no spell slots or mana.
  • Blast and Healing may spend extra actions for +2 on their roll per action. Controlling gains +2 spell DC per extra action. Total commitment is at most three actions. Tier never changes that price or bonus.
  • Each selected target must meet the tree’s range and ordinary line-of-sight rules. A caster may receive its own beneficial magic. A multi-target spell affects each recipient once. Choose all targets before rolling.
  • Beneficial spells affect surviving units, including undead troops and units that have routed. Restoring at least 1 Morale ends a rout. Destroyed units remain destroyed.
  • Spell buffs follow these stacking rules. Recasting the same active effect grants no additional benefit. Different effects can coexist. Ward and Sure strike cancel on the same attack. Sanctuary counts as Stoneskin, and Battle chorus counts as Sure strike for these rules.

Except for Haste and instantaneous transfers, a self-cast buff expires at the end of the caster’s current activation; a buff on another unit expires at the end of that unit’s next activation. Ward, Stoneskin, Aegis, and Sanctuary instead protect a self-caster through the end of its next activation. Each recipient tracks its own expiry.

Battlefield medicine grants Soothe using Will. Demoralize grants Dread using the troop’s level DC. These tactics grant only those spells.

Siege engines

Control and capture

Place engines on dry hexes before deployment. A unit on an engine claims and operates it. An adjacent ally reserves an empty engine but must enter its hex to use it.

Entering an engine’s hex captures it immediately. At round end, an empty engine belongs to the only side with units beside it. If both sides are beside an unclaimed engine, it stays unclaimed.

Capture preserves loading progress and the engine’s attack for the round. An engine that already fired waits until the next round to attack again.

Attacks

Roll d20 + engine launch bonus − crew’s missing Morale, with crew modifiers and extra commitment. Compare with each target’s Defence. Use the activity’s damage and effects. Damage triggers a Fortitude save against the engine’s level DC. Guard and Stoneskin damage caps still apply.

Each engine attacks once per round. The crew may operate it while engaged, subject to the activity’s target and range restrictions. Area attacks also hit allies; check the preview.

Siege range uses fixed limits: short 3, medium 5, long 7, extreme 11 hexes. Each activity may specify a minimum or a smaller area. The whole area needs range and sight. High-angle attacks ignore cover but still need sight.

Damage in the catalogue reads hit/critical hit. Wall attacks use structural damage and penetration; see Attacking walls.

Loading

Engines start loaded unless setup says otherwise. Fill every loading pip to prepare an attack. Firing empties the pips. You may Load while engaged, after attacking, or before firing if enough actions remain.

Rams and the Bolt Emitter need no reload. Full-rack and overcharged attacks include extra preparation in their attack cost.

Special effects

Follow each activity’s listed effects and duration. Forced movement respects barriers, occupied hexes, roots, Take cover and hauling. Some attacks affect only cavalry, units in water or shallows, or ground troops.

Debris and web fields cost at least 2 movement to enter and stop charges through the end of the following round. Webs let infantry cross a wall between two webbed hexes if they haul no engine. Fire clears webs. A web hit prevents movement through the target’s next activation. All fields clear overnight.

Hauling and abandonment

Haul requires freedom from enemy contact, pinning and roots. It clears unused movement. A unit hauls one engine at a time. Moving without hauling, or by magical transfer, leaves the engine behind.

Use the slower of crew and engine Speed. Convert engine Speed from feet to hexes by dividing by 15 and rounding up to a whole hex, as for troops. Every mobile engine has at least Speed 1. At Speed 1, entering rough ground one level uphill costs two actions, and entering swamp costs three. Portable engines use crew Speed. Fixed engines stay in place.

Destruction or rout abandons the engine in its current hex. An enemy beside an abandoned engine at battle end captures it.

Dusk and recovery

At round end, the battle ends if a side has no standing units. A standing unit is alive, on the field and above 0 Morale.

Otherwise, dusk ends the day at the round limit: six by default, or eight in setup. Resolve pending persistent damage once before counting survivors. Both sides withdraw from contact; the field remains contested.

Survivors

The report lists each unit’s Health, Morale and outcome. Destroyed units are permanently lost. Units that leave routed miss overnight recovery and the next day. Units that enter camp without routing remain eligible.

Choose End battle to return to setup, or Continue to recovery to resolve the night. Continuing the fight requires standing survivors on both sides.

Overnight recovery

  1. For each survivor, choose None, Rally or Treat Wounded. Each unit may attempt one activity.
  2. Declare all participants for your side before rolling. The first adds no penalty. Each extra participant gives −2 to every recovery check on that side.
  3. Choose Roll recovery. Resolve each check once. Each side rolls separately, in either order.
  4. Review the results, then choose orders.

RallyWill against the level DC of the highest-level enemy still standing for the next dayRestore 1 MoraleRestore 2 Morale

Roll d20 + save − missing Morale − 2 × (participants − 1). Count both activities together. One participant gives 0 penalty; two give −2; three give −4.

Failure and critical failure change nothing. Recovery stops at 4 Health or 3 Morale. A unit may choose only a statistic below its maximum. Each result is final for that night.

Orders after recovery

OrderOutcome
Propose surrenderThe opponent accepts or rejects. Acceptance makes the opponent victorious; players agree campaign terms. Rejection returns you to the choice of orders.
WithdrawLeave with surviving troops at current Health and Morale, without a check or extra casualties. If the enemy holds, they win. If both withdraw, the field remains contested and the battle ends.
Hold the fieldIf both hold, fight another day. If the enemy withdraws, you win the field.

Resolve surrender proposals, then confirm both sides’ decisions. Changing orders grants no further recovery. Recovery results stand even if a side withdraws.

Next day

If both sides hold, choose Fight another day, then Same map or New map. A new map lets you choose terrain, features and fortifications before deployment.

  • Keep Health and Morale after recovery. Temporary conditions and spells expire. Actions and engine attacks reset.
  • On the same map, breaches persist and emplacements keep position and ownership.
  • On a new map, fixed emplacements and abandoned equipment stay on the old field. The battle record retains them for the campaign.
  • Redeploy survivors in their home zones. Engines still attached to their crews move with them. Equipment losses remain.
  • Begin at round 1. Keep earlier days and recovery rolls in the log.

Campaign results

Demoralized = 3 − Morale. Import and export the same track, including recovery. Apply its penalty once. Convert Health to hit points using the Health table. Rejoin split armies using Setup.

In ReignMaker, an army that leaves through rout keeps Routed. Rally Troops costs one leader action and a leadership skill check at −5. Success removes Routed and sets Demoralized to 2 (1 Morale), preserving Health. Failure leaves Routed in place.

At the end of the ReignMaker turn, every army still Routed disbands permanently. A battlefield day does not advance that turn.

Captured engines change owner; abandoned engines are lost. The loser’s survivors retreat one campaign hex toward their nearest friendly settlement. Fame, Unrest and other kingdom effects use campaign actions.

Battle tactics

  • Protect Morale. Rally before a unit routs. Use allies to recover a unit at 0 Morale.
  • Use spare actions. After an attack, Guard or Rally. Extra commitment improves an important roll.
  • Outflank. Engage a target with at least two units to reduce its Defence by 2.
  • Choose targets by saves. Fortitude resists Morale loss from damage. Will resists repulse and hostile Morale magic.
  • Use terrain. Cover protects defenders. Cliffs, water and walls restrict approaches. Keep a retreat route open.
  • Watch the clock. Dusk limits the attacker’s time. Higher levels improve attacks and make resistance harder.
