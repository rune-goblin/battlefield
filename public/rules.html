Field Battles

Rules for resolving a battle between armies in a single sitting.

In one paragraph. Each army is a unit on a hexagon of sixty-one hexes. The sides alternate: one unit activates and takes three actions, then the other side answers. A unit does one of five things — Shoot, Fight, Guard, Rally, Cast — or it moves. Shoot, Fight, Guard and Rally are each a ladder of three rungs, and every ladder is priced the same way: the rung at a unit's grade costs one action, and each rung above it costs one action more. Nothing is rolled for a rung. Cast has no grade: its three tiers cost one, two and three actions (section 11). Actions left over buy other acts, never a bonus. An attack is one roll: a hit deals wounds, one or two, and four wounds destroy a unit. A wound costs the target disorder unless its Fortitude saves, and a Fight that misses costs the attacker disorder unless its Will saves. A unit whose disorder reaches its Quality is shaken, and one point past it routs. The battle ends when one side has nothing standing, or at dusk after six rounds.

Quick reference

1. What you need

Each army's troop sheet, a d20, and the battle board in the kingdom app: a hexagon of sixty-one hexes, files lettered a to i and ranks numbered 1 to 9, generated from the hex being fought over. Each player commands the armies they lead and any the GM hands them. The GM commands every army of an enemy faction.

A battle happens when armies of hostile factions end up in the same hex. The side that moved in is the attacker; the side already there is the defender. The defender's hex sets the ground (section 10). The battle resolves inside the kingdom's Actions phase, before the next kingdom action.

2. The unit

An army in battle is a unit, and a unit is one of two types: infantry or cavalry. A siege engine is never a unit; it rides with one or holds a hex of its own (section 12). You never invent a number for a unit. Every value comes off the troop sheet, and a card with no sheet takes the level tables for its type.

Reflex is the stat that tells troops apart: it spreads 5.6 points within a level where Armour Class spreads 3.2, which is why breaking contact reads off Reflex rather than off Defence. Perception is derived and displayed, but no rule reads it — there is no initiative roll (section 5). Fortitude now reads for one thing: the save a wound from a shot, a Blast or a free strike asks before it disorders anyone (section 8).

A card with no sheet takes the level tables for its type. Infantry: high AC, moderate attack, high Will, moderate Reflex, moderate Fortitude, one hex an action. Cavalry: high AC, high attack, moderate Will, high Reflex, moderate Fortitude, two hexes an action.

Grades

A unit carries a grade of 1, 2 or 3 on each of four ladders. The grade is the rung a unit takes for one action; each rung above it costs one more (section 6). The grade is read off the statblock, never hand-picked. Cast carries no grade of its own — a tradition caps which tiers a caster may buy instead (section 11).

LadderGrade comes from

Guard1, plus 1 for fighting in formation, plus 1 for shields or a magic ward. Maximum 3.

An importer reads melee drill, shields, formation, magic ward, mounted and no retreat straight off the recurring action names on a statblock. A trained tactic, where a card carries one, raises one grade to 3 and does nothing else: covering fire raises Shoot; a reactive attack, dirty fighting or a feint raises Fight; raised shields or a shield block raises Guard; defending allies or battlefield medicine raises Rally.

Armour Class and attack DC carry no grade information

Across all 162 published troops, Armour Class spreads 3.2 points within a level and attack DC 2.6 — at level 12 every troop shares one attack DC. Both are functions of level, so neither says anything about a troop's character. Will spreads 5.1 and Speed falls across seven bands, so those two are read numerically, and the recurring action names carry the rest.

Quality

A unit's Quality is the disorder it absorbs before it is shaken, and it too comes off the Will save: 2 below the low band, 3 at low, 4 at moderate, 5 at high, 6 at extreme. Line Infantry has Quality 3; Kobold Warriors, better disciplined for their level, have 4; Troll Marauders, who are not disciplined at all, have 2 and are shaken by two bad Fights.

3. The battlefield

The battlefield is a hexagon of sixty-one hexes, in rows of 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 8, 7, 6 and 5. Files run a to i and ranks 1 to 9, from the attacker's edge to the defender's; e5 is the centre. Name a hex by file and rank: c3, e7. Every unit stands on one hex, and a hex holds at most one unit.

Hexes have no diagonal. Every one of a hex's six neighbours is adjacent, at distance 1, and the distance between two units is the number of steps from one hex to the other. Distance sets the band a shot falls in:

DistanceBandNote
1EngagedFight. No shooting either way.

7–8ExtremeNo troop's own Salvo is anchored here — only a siege engine's effective range starts this far out
9 or moreBeyondNever occurs on this board: extreme's own ceiling is already the farthest two hexes can be

Which rung reaches a given band depends on the shooter, not the band. Fire is a weapon's own effective range (its Reach, section 2); Aim swings one band off it and Snipe two, either direction, whichever way the target actually is — each a rung above, priced as section 6 prices every rung. A short-reach troop's Snipe can still reach as far as long, but no further — extreme takes a medium or long effective range aiming out, or a siege engine's own reach, which starts there.

Two enemy units on adjacent hexes are engaged, unless a cliff lies on the edge between them. A unit engaged with two or more enemies is outflanked and takes −2 to Defence. An engaged unit cannot stride away and cannot shoot; it fights, guards, rallies, casts, or withdraws (section 7).

Hexes carry terrain and height; edges carry walls and cliffs (section 10).

4. Setting up

Setup runs in five stages, each on its own screen in the app.

Stage 1: the battlefield

Pick the hex's terrain (plains, forest, hills, mountains, swamp or desert), add a feature if the hex has one (a river or a lakeside) and a construction if it holds one (a fort of tier 0 to 3), then roll a seed. The generator lays the board out, and the same seed always gives the same board. Reroll until it looks like the hex.

Terrain is a tendency, not a rule: a plains board can carry a copse and a hills board a marsh, never many. Hills and desert raise a low ridge, mountains a high one with peaks. A river runs across ranks 4 to 6, the band neither side deploys on, and carries one or two fords; a lake hugs one flank with a marshy shore. Water never lands on a deployment rank, so both sides can always deploy.

Hex terrainWhat the generator lays down

Stage 2: paint

The GM adjusts the board by hand: set any hex's terrain, set its height to 0, 1 or 2, put a wall on any edge, or clear a hex. This is where a hazard from the adventure goes — the bridge, the burning barn, the ravine.

Stages 3 and 4: the two forces

Build each side from the troop library, the force generator or a custom card, attach siege engines to the units that bring them, then put every unit on a hex. The attacker deploys anywhere on ranks 1 to 3, the defender anywhere on ranks 7 to 9, never on water. A unit with an ambush tactic may deploy one rank further in. Both sides need at least one unit, and every unit needs a hex. Note each unit's wounds and disorder from its sheet.

Three ranks lie between the lines. A unit on rank 3 reaches contact in its first activation; a unit held back on rank 1 takes two. The depth of your deployment is your first decision.

Every player commands a unit. When the players' side brings fewer armies than players, split armies before deployment: one army becomes two or more units of the same card, each with its own hex, wounds and disorder. Each half is a full unit, a separate target for outflanking and a separate candidate for routing; a player who commands one may lose it. After the battle the halves rejoin: wounds take the average rounded up, disorder the higher. The GM may also hand an allied faction's unit to a player for the battle.

The opposing force. The GM picks the enemy from the library or lets the generator build one. The generator reads the other side and answers it: the same number of units give or take one, every unit within three levels of the other side's average, and the total level within a tenth of the other side's. At most one unit brings a siege engine. An attacker facing walls brings one three times in four, and it may be a ram; anyone else brings artillery one time in five. Add to the generated force or take from it.

Stage 5: begin

The attacker's side activates first. There is no initiative roll.

5. The round

A round is an hour of battle, and every unit acts once in it. The sides alternate: after each activation, the side with more units still to act goes next, and on a tie the side that did not act last. You choose which of your own units takes each of your turns.

An activation is three actions. A unit attacks once per activation — one Fight, one Shoot, or one Blast. Further actions buy other acts, or a higher rung of this one, never a second attack.

When every unit has acted, the round ends:

  1. Each side that has lost half its starting units to death or rout takes it once, that round only: every unit of that side still standing gains 1 disorder.
  2. An emplaced engine with only the enemy beside it changes hands. Every engine reloads.
  3. If either side has no unit still standing, the battle ends. If this was the sixth round, dusk ends it.

Alternating activation is what keeps a round readable: no side ever takes its whole army's worth of actions while the other watches, and a side that outnumbers the other spends its surplus at the end of the round rather than the start.

6. Ladders, grades and price

Four things a unit can do this way, and each is a ladder of three rungs. The rung is what happens; the grade is how high the unit climbs for one action. A fifth, Cast, works differently — no grade, and a tradition-capped tree instead of a rung (section 11). Each rung includes everything below it.

The menu is filtered by where the unit stands, so it is never long. In contact: Fight and Guard. In the open: Shoot and Guard. A shaken unit is offered Rally alone, and a routed one no ladder at all; both may still Move and withdraw. Rally is otherwise always offered, and a caster adds a row for each of the six trees its tradition grants. An attacker beside a wall may Fight the wall even with nobody defending it.

The price: how each ladder gets above its grade

Every ladder pays the same way. A rung at or below the grade costs one action. One rung above costs two, and two above costs three. Nothing is rolled for a rung, and nothing converts an action into a bonus: what an action buys is always a different effect, never a bigger number on the same one. This is Pathfinder's own shape — the one-, two- and three-action versions of Heal — and it makes a grade what it should be. A grade-3 troop takes the whole ladder for one action and has two left for other acts. A grade-1 troop can reach the same rung, and it costs it the activation.

GradeRung 1Rung 2Rung 3

So a three-action activation is a plan made of two or three named things: Press and Brace, or Strike, Brace and Rally, or Overrun alone. The third action on this ladder competes with the third action on every other verb, and that is the whole decision. A compelled unit (section 11) may not pay for a rung above its grade.

One attack an activation. Fight, Shoot and a Blast are the same slot. A second attack was the thing that broke the pacing, so the spare actions buy other acts instead. Everything else — Guard, Rally, the other spells, moving — may be repeated.

7. Moving and breaking away

Move

One Move action buys the unit's hexes (the row above), and a hex costs that budget by its terrain: 1 for open ground or a settlement, 2 for forest or shallows, 3 for swamp, plus 1 for each level of height climbed. Water cannot be entered at all. So a 1-hex-an-action infantry troop spends two actions to enter forest and three to enter swamp, and a 2-hex-an-action cavalry troop spends one and two.

Movement banks across the whole activation rather than lapsing at the end of each Move, so a 2-hex troop that steps one open hex keeps the leftover hex for the next step. A unit may not enter an occupied hex, cross a standing wall, or cross a cliff. A breach in a wall is an ordinary crossing.

A flier pays 1 a hex whatever the ground and crosses walls and cliffs. Flight buys no extra distance: how far a unit goes is still its Speed.

Charge

A charge is the movement it takes plus the Fight rung's own price, and the melee is an ordinary Fight. It is one act, so it spends the activation's attack.

Withdraw

Withdrawing is the only way out of contact. A shaken or routed unit in the open Moves like any other; in contact it withdraws like any other. It costs one action, and it is not a ladder: it is one Disengage check, however many enemies hold you — d20 + Reflex, less disorder, against the highest attack DC among them, which is a holder's Strike plus 10. The one roll is read for every holder. An enemy with no melee attack holds nobody.

DisengageResult
Critical successClean break, and a free Move of your Speed. Every pursuer that gives no retreat is thrown off outright.
SuccessClean break, one hex clear
FailureEvery holder lands a free strike: one wound at most, never a critical
Critical failureThe free strikes, 1 disorder, and their grip holds — you stay where you stand

Having broken away, the unit moves one hex clear of everything it was engaged with, and each further action put on the run buys another Speed's worth of ground. A unit with nowhere clear to go is offered the rest rather than being stuck. A troop that gives no retreat follows: one free move of its own Speed to re-establish contact, dealing no damage. Outrunning it is the only way clear.

8. Wounds

Damage is never rolled. A hit deals one wound, a critical hit two, and each unit holds four. The level difference between attacker and target does the work damage dice do elsewhere: a level-6 troop striking a level-2 troop hits on about 7 and criticals from 17, and the reverse hits only from 17 and cannot critical without help.

A unit that enters battle already hurt starts with wounds: 0 above three-quarters of its hit points, 1 above half, 2 above a quarter, 3 with any left. After the battle they are written back the same way.

The attack

Every attack is one roll, one way. A Fight is d20 + Strike against the target's Defence; a shot is d20 + Volley the same way; a Blast is the caster's spell attack. Critical success deals 2 wounds, success 1, failure nothing. Nobody strikes back: the target's answer is its own activation.

Both saves are d20 + the save, less disorder, against the other unit's level DC. Fortitude for the wound and Will for the repulse: one is a body flinching from a hit, the other a line losing heart. A missed shot or Blast repulses nobody — the shooter is not in contact. A free strike on a failed withdrawal is a hit capped at one wound, with the Fortitude save as usual.

Press skips the target's save: a hit disorders it outright. A Press that misses is an ordinary repulse, so there is always a risk to engaging. Overrun is Press, and a hit drives the target one hex directly away from you; you step into the hex it left, so contact holds. Water, a wall, a cliff, an occupied hex or the board's edge on that one hex blocks the shove, and a target that cannot give ground holds and takes 1 more disorder instead. A target the attack destroys simply yields its hex. A Defense buff that takes a wound with no disorder (section 11) still beats a Press.

Shooting

The rung sets how far off the shooter's own effective range the shot reaches. Fire is that range itself; Aim swings one band off it and Snipe two, in whichever direction the target actually is — nearer or farther. Nothing about the rung touches cover. Shooting is not possible while engaged, in either direction.

Defence

Every attack rolls against the target's Defence, and Defence is built the same way each time.

A unit that has dug in caps every hit it takes at one wound, so a critical against it lands as an ordinary one.

9. Disorder and rout

Morale is one number: disorder. Each point is −1 to everything the unit rolls and −1 to its Defence. Morale runs one point past Quality, and the two bands above it are different things.

When disorder reaches the unit's Quality (section 2) the unit is shaken: it may do nothing but Rally, Move or withdraw. It holds its hex if it wants to, it still counts as standing, and Rally is a real way back — a shaken unit that clears a point is a unit again.

One point past Quality and it is routed: nothing but Move and the withdrawal, and it leaves the field when it reaches its own edge. A withdrawal it makes runs for that edge. Disorder stops there; a routed unit cannot be driven further. Its own Rally is gone, so the only way back is an adjacent ally's Rally or an Inspire within 2 — which is what makes the troop beside a breaking one worth keeping there.

Rally

Every unit that is not routed may Rally, whatever else its situation allows: in contact or in the open, in good order or shaken. A shaken unit has Rally, Move and the withdrawal and nothing else, which is what makes the act its way back. The rout is the only thing that takes it away, and then only the unit's own — an ally's Rally still reaches it.

Rally is the only thing that clears disorder. Roll d20 + Will, less disorder, against the rout DC: the level DC of the highest-level enemy within short range, or, when no enemy is that close, of the highest-level enemy on the field. Rallying under a dragon's eye is harder than rallying beside a levy.

The roll says how much clears; the rung says who else it reaches. Every rung lets an ally take heart — +2 on its attacks until it has acted — and from Rally upward the ally clears a point as well. This is why a unit with nothing to clear still has a use for the act: a levy's Steady is a poor self-rally and a real gift to the troop beside it.

10. Ground, height and walls

Three layers shape the field. Terrain fills a hex and changes what checks cost; height decides who has the advantage; barriers sit on edges and decide where units may go. Forest hides, swamp slows, water blocks, height commands, walls hold.

Height

Every hex stands at height 0, 1 or 2. Count the difference between the two hexes:

  • Attacking upward, in melee or with a shot, costs −1 for each level of difference.
  • Shooting downward counts the range one band closer, and the target's forest cover does not apply.
  • Climbing costs 1 a level on top of the terrain.
  • An edge with a difference of 2 is a cliff: nobody crosses it, and nobody is engaged across it.

Walls

A wall sits on the edge between two hexes. Nobody crosses it until it is breached, but units on either side are engaged across it and shots cross it freely. An attacker striking across a standing wall takes −2. A defender standing beside a wall of its own is garrisoned and shoots at +1.

Only the attacker attacks a wall, and each segment has wound boxes equal to its tier plus one. Fight a segment on an edge of your own hex, or bombard one within your rung's band with artillery. The DC is 10 + the wall's tier + the level of the highest defender still standing; a hit takes one box, a critical two, and a segment with no boxes left is breached and gone. A ram rolls at +2 and is good for nothing else. Walls are repaired through fortification maintenance, never during a battle.

A fort is a block of settlement hexes against the defender's edge, walled on its front and flanks with the segments its tier allows: four edges at tier 0, up to seven at tier 3. A short budget leaves a gate open on one flank. Whatever the fortification gives the garrison in Defence and saves is already on its sheet.

11. Magic

A unit whose statblock names a spellcasting entry is a caster, and every caster belongs to one of magic's four traditions: arcane, divine, occult, primal. A tradition does not carry every kind of magic — see Tradition, below.

Casting

A caster carries two more numbers on its sheet, alongside Battle DC and Salvo DC: spell attack, what the caster rolls to attack with Blast, and spell DC, what a target must beat to resist Controlling. Neither is Will or the level-DC table doing double duty — they're the caster's own imported numbers, the same way Strike and Battle DC already are.

A cast is priced like every other ladder: Tier 1 of whichever tree you choose costs one action, Tier 2 two and Tier 3 three, at the tree's own base range (below). A tier above the first buys one of three things — extended range, a longer duration, or a stronger effect — and never more than one on a single cast. Nothing is rolled to reach a tier.

Once the cast lands, an enemy-targeted tree — Blast or Controlling — needs a second, separate effect roll to see how well it bites, decoupled from the cast roll that got it there. Blast is a spell attack: the caster rolls d20 + spell attack, less disorder, against the target's Defence. It is still the activation's one attack, the same slot a Fight or a Shot uses. Controlling remains resistance: the target rolls d20 + Will, less disorder, against the caster's spell DC. An ally-targeted tree — Healing, or the Offense, Defense or Movement buff — has nothing to resist, so it simply applies once the cast lands, no second roll.

A caster also carries actions of its own that nothing but a cast can spend: level ÷ 5, rounded down — 0 below level 5, rising to 4 at level 20. They refresh every activation, exactly as the ordinary three do, and they go first, so a Tier 3 cast leaves a level-10 caster one ordinary action for something else. Cast carries no grade: the tradition's cap (below) is the only thing deciding which tiers are for sale.

The six trees

A tier is a different kind of thing, not a bigger number on the same thing — the same rule every rung already follows. Cast · range and Cast · duration aren't trees a tradition gates; every caster buys them the same way regardless of tradition. 12. Siege engines

An engine is attached to a unit and rides with it, or emplaced on a hex of its own. An emplacement is worked by whichever friendly unit stands in or beside it, and it needs no crew of its own; leave it alone and it is abandoned where it stands.

A loaded artillery piece replaces its unit's shooting profile, effective range and all: the unit shoots with the engine's launch bonus, from the engine's own Reach rather than its own, and always at grade 3 — a gun crew works its whole engineered spread without paying the actions an ordinary troop spends climbing to the same band. An engine's Reach may be extreme, which no troop's own Salvo ever is; that is what siege equipment is for. No engine may fire while its crew is engaged, because nobody shoots in contact. A ram fights walls at +2 and does nothing else. Each engine fires once a round, whoever works it; where two friendly units both stand beside an emplacement, the crew is the first in deployment order.

An emplaced engine never moves, so an emplaced ram can only ever batter a wall on an edge of the hex it deployed in. A ram is normally attached.

A unit that is destroyed or routs abandons its engines where it stands. An emplacement left with only the enemy beside it changes hands at the end of the round — the engine is taken by standing on it, not by winning a fight over it — and an abandoned engine beside an enemy still standing at the end of the battle is captured.

13. Ending the battle

The battle ends at the end of a round: when a side has no unit still standing, or after the sixth round, whichever comes first. A unit is standing when it is neither destroyed nor routed nor gone from the field; a shaken unit still counts, so a side reduced to shaken units has not yet lost.

Dusk

If both sides still stand after six rounds, both withdraw, keep their wounds and disorder, and the hex stays contested.

The kingdom's checks

What a battle's outcome buys the kingdom — Fame, Unrest, recovery — is not these rules' concern. A battle ends at wounds, disorder, destroyed and captured; what a wider campaign does with that result is a kingdom action of its own.

Back on the kingdom map

Wounds become hit points on the same four thresholds (section 8). Disorder becomes the army's demoralized value, keeping whichever is higher; an army at demoralized 3 disbands at upkeep unless it is rallied or supported. Split armies rejoin (section 4). Destroyed units disband; a captured engine changes owner and an abandoned one is lost. The loser's surviving units fall back one hex toward their nearest friendly settlement.

14. Fighting against the odds

There are no points. Four things decide an uneven fight:

  • Level. Two levels of difference is noticeable; five is decisive. Level sets the DC of the saves made against you: a wound from a high-level troop is harder to shrug off, and a line it repulses is harder to steady.
  • Quality. A troop with a poor Will save is shaken at 2 disorder, which is two disordering hits, and routed at 3. Trolls hit harder than almost anything at their level and break faster than a militia.
  • Numbers. Outflanking pays only when it reaches a second side of the enemy, and a ridge or a river decides where that second side is. Three lesser units grind down one greater one through disorder, not through wounds.
  • Ground and time. Forest, high ground and walls favour the defender. Six rounds and the half-army check reward a decisive attacker and punish a hesitant one.

A side whose total levels are less than half the enemy's should hold ground or refuse battle.

15. Proposed ladders, under review

Nothing in this section is in force. Section 6 is the rule and the engine plays it. What follows is a proposal for every ladder the Fight rework left standing, written in the same shape so it reads against them. Each ladder is judged by the four tests from the Fight review: a rung buys a different kind of thing, never a bigger number; one roll gates each effect; attacking down is nearly free, even costs something, up is a mistake; six rounds can decide it. Under each table: the cells that changed, the Pathfinder ancestor, and the edge cases. Rungs stay cumulative and priced as section 6 prices them.

Shoot

Today the rungs buy range: Fire is the effective range, Aim one band off it and Snipe two, either direction. That is three prices for one thing. Range comes off the ladder and onto the roll, which is Pathfinder's own rule, and the rungs buy what volume fire does to the troop under it.

RungCostWhat it does
Fire◆A volley at any target you can see, at −2 for every band beyond your effective range. Nearer bands cost nothing.
Suppress◆◆Fire, and hit or miss the target is suppressed: −2 to everything it rolls until the start of your next activation.
Pin◆◆◆Suppress, and the target is pinned: until your next activation you count as one of its holders, at your attack DC, Volley + 10. To leave its hex it must Withdraw as if in contact, and a failure lands no free strike from you, since nobody is in contact.
  • Changed: every cell. Aim and Snipe go, and with them the swing nearer, the three-action shot at a target two hexes away. Height counting a band closer becomes a −2 not taken. Artillery already shoots at grade 3 from its own reach, so a gun crew Pins for one action.
  • Ancestors: the range-increment rule, −2 per increment beyond the first, is Fire's penalty verbatim; −4 was considered and would put a medium-reach troop at −8 into extreme, a shot nobody takes. The volley trait charges a −2 for shooting too near, on the weapons that carry it, so nearness is a unit ability if it is anything and no troop pays it here. Frightened is a status penalty to every check that fades on its own: Suppress. Pin has none in ranged form; Tumble Through's failure, the move ending where the check failed, is its shape.
  • Suppress bites hit or miss because volume fire works by volume; a rung that only lands on a hit is Press again. Suppressed is disorder's temporary cousin, two points that lift on their own, and the two stack.
  • Pin was first drafted as outright: the target may not Move or Charge. Mark: "It's probably just too strong a control. I would make them roll as if they needed to disengage, even though it's a ranged attack." Then: "if a pin effect is being applied by a shoot action, that's just counted as one of the holders." So a pinned unit is held, its one Disengage check is against the highest DC among everything holding it, and the shooter is one of the holders the Disengage rung makes roll. It is Reflex against an attack DC, as breaking contact is; nothing here asks for a skill a troop may not carry.
  • A unit with the covering-fire tactic sits at grade 3 and Pins for one action, which is the first time the tactic's name has described its effect.

Guard

Today every rung is +2 Defence, Dig in blunts criticals and roots, and Shieldwall braces the neighbours. The +2 stays, and it is the one exception to the rule against numbers: a raised shield is nothing but a harder unit to hit, and cover is the same thing again. The rungs above it are Pathfinder's own two grades of cover, and the sharing goes to the troops built for it.

RungCostWhat it does
Brace◆Shields up: +2 Defence until you next act.
Dig in◆◆Brace, and standard cover: every hit against you lands as an ordinary hit, a critical capped at one wound.
Take cover◆◆◆Dig in, and greater cover: +4 Defence in place of the +2, and you hold your ground: an Overrun cannot drive you back. You may not move again this activation.
  • Changed: Brace is what it is today. Dig in loses the root. Take cover replaces Shieldwall: it is the turtle, the whole activation spent on defence, and the root is the price of the ground it keeps. Nothing on the ladder reaches a neighbour.
  • Ancestors: Raise a Shield is a circumstance bonus to AC: Brace. Shield Block absorbs a hit: the cap. Take Cover grants standard cover, +2 to AC, or greater cover where you already had standard, +4: Dig in and Take cover, by name. The ancestor's Reflex bonus against area effects is not carried over: a Blast resolves as an attack roll against Defence, so the Defence bonus already answers it.
  • The share becomes a unit ability. Adjacency is not something every troop can count on, so bracing the neighbours belongs to the troops built for it. The defend allies tactic grants it: that unit's Guard, at any rung, gives every adjacent ally what Brace buys. The one roster troop with the tactic is Shield Wall, which carries raised shields and a shield block besides, and that is the unit the old rung was describing. Formation, which 31 roster troops carry, keeps raising the grade and grants nothing else. Shield Warden, the feat that extends a Shield Block to an adjacent ally, is the ancestor.
  • The cap touches the wound and nothing else, so a Press against a dug-in unit still lands its point. Against a unit under cover an Overrun is a Press: the shove fails, the attacker stays where it is, and the target takes nothing for it.
  • Two other rungs were drafted and dropped: a hit turned outright ("complete immunity to the next hit sounds too strong"), and a repulse with no save against a Fight that misses you.
  • Guard lasts until the unit next acts, as today, and the root with it.

Rally

The roll stays and the rung carries the scope. Roll d20 + Will, less disorder, against the rout DC as section 9 has it. A success is one effect, and it is what every rung hands on: a unit with disorder clears 1, and a unit with none is inspired, +2 to its next roll.

RungCostWhat it does
Steady◆Roll. On a success you clear 1 of your own disorder, or if you have none, you are inspired.
Rally◆◆Steady, and the same result for one adjacent ally: it clears 1, or if it has none, it is inspired.
Inspire◆◆◆Rally, and the same result for every friendly unit within 2.
RallyEach unit reached
Critical successClears 2, and if none is left, it is inspired
SuccessClears 1, or if it has none, it is inspired
FailureNothing
Critical failureNothing, and the rallying unit takes 1 more
  • Changed: the grid. Section 9 clears all on a critical success, 2 on a success and 1 on a failure. Now a success clears 1 or inspires, a failure is nothing, and the critical is 2. A unit cannot be inspired while it still carries disorder, so Rally on a shaken line steadies it and Rally on a steady line lifts it.
  • One roll. The rallying unit rolls once and the result is read for every unit the rung reaches, as Burst reads one attack against every neighbour. Today the ally's clear is the rung's gift with no roll; now the roll gates it. The extra point on a critical failure lands on the rallying unit alone.
  • Inspired: you are inspired, +2 to your next roll. It was +2 on the unit's attacks until it had acted; now it is one roll of any kind, the first the unit makes: an attack, a save against disorder, a Disengage check. It is a status bonus, so it does not stack with another status bonus to the same roll. It lapses when the roll is made or when the unit has acted, whichever is first, and a second Rally on an inspired unit adds nothing.
  • Ancestors: Heal, one action on one target, three actions on everyone around you. Courageous Anthem gives allies +1 to attacks and to saves against fear; Rallying Anthem +1 AC and saves. Both are status bonuses. Inspired is the same kind of thing, on one roll instead of a duration.
  • Whether the third rung keeps the name Inspire, now that the condition is called inspired, is open.

Withdraw

Ground is never a rung: once clear, a unit Moves like any other, so a rung that bought a Speed bought nothing. The rungs change who rolls. At the first the withdrawing unit rolls to get away; above it, it simply goes, and the enemy left holding air rolls instead.

RungCostWhat it does
Break off◆One Disengage check against the highest holder, as section 7 has it. On a success, one hex clear of everything holding you; on a critical, a free Move of your Speed.
Disengage◆◆No check and no free strike: you are one hex clear. Instead each holder rolls d20 + Reflex, less disorder, against your level DC. On a failure it may not Move, Charge or Withdraw on its next activation, and it does not follow.
Fighting retreat◆◆◆Disengage, and a holder that fails its roll takes 1 disorder as well, drawn out of its line.
  • Changed: Fall back and Flee are gone. Section 7's ground-buying actions go with them, since a shaken or routed unit now Moves. Disengage is the sure break: two actions for the ground, and the roll passes to the enemy. Fighting retreat adds one thing, a point of disorder on the holder that fails, which is the Fight's repulse from the other side.
  • One roll. At Break off the withdrawing unit rolls once, whatever holds it, and the roll is read for every holder: a failure is a free strike from each. Above it, each holder rolls once, and both rungs read off that one roll: rooted at Disengage, rooted and disordered at Fighting retreat. Nothing rolls twice.
  • Grade: 1 for every troop, 2 for a troop with pace, so cavalry and fliers Disengage for one action.
  • Ancestors: Trip is the shape of the enemy rolling: a check against the target's Reflex DC, and a failure leaves it unable to move until it acts. Rooted is the word Guard and Hold already use. Reactive Strike is the free strike, and it stays the one reaction in the game. Break off makes the Disengage check of section 7; the rung named Disengage is the one that needs no check, as Rally's rung 2 shares the ladder's name.
  • The critical. Break off is the gamble and Disengage the sure thing, so the critical is where the ground is: a free Move of your Speed, the one way a rung of Withdraw ever buys distance. Nothing rolled for a rung and nothing for sale.
  • A pin is a holder. A unit under Pin (Shoot, above) counts the shooter among what holds it, at Volley + 10, and leaves its hex by this ladder. The shooter lands no free strike. At Disengage and Fighting retreat the shooter rolls like any holder, and a failure roots it.
  • A troop that gives no retreat still follows a Break off as section 7 has it. Against Disengage it follows only if it passes its roll.

Charge

Charge is for everyone. Today a charge is a Move plus the Fight rung, priced separately, so it is a click and nothing else. The charge becomes one act with a discount on the approach and an edge on arrival: one action of movement buys up to two Speeds provided it ends in a Fight, the Fight is at +2, and the charging unit is exposed afterwards. It is Sudden Charge's shape and the wargame's: every unit charges, impetus for the charger, a looser line once it is in, and cavalry differs by reach and by impact, never by owning the verb.

RungCostWhat it does
Charge◆◆Up to two Speeds of movement, at the ordinary terrain prices, ending in contact, then a Strike at +2. You are exposed until you next act. A miss repulses like any Fight. It is the activation's attack.
Charge and Press◆◆◆Charge, then Press instead of Strike: a hit's disorder needs no save.
Charge and Overrun◆◆◆◆Charge, then Overrun: a hit's disorder needs no save, and it drives them one hex straight back; you take their ground. Four actions, so only a troop with an extra action (below) can buy it.
  • Changed: two Moves and a Strike cost two actions instead of three, the roll is at +2, and the charger pays for it with its Defence. Prices are flat because grades are gone (below): the movement is one action and the Fight rung costs its own number.
  • The exception. The +2 is a flat bonus, and Charge is the second ladder after Guard allowed one: what a charge is, in every wargame that has one, is a bigger swing, and the effect-shaped version of hitting harder is already Press. Mark: "It's always easier to increase than to take things away." A −2 on the target's save against disorder was proposed and dropped from the plain charge, since with the +2 it doubled up on the same hit; it lives on the downhill charge instead.
  • Exposed is section 6's own condition, −2 Defence until the unit acts again, the one a critically failed Strike hands out. A charge gives it to the charger in contact with the unit about to act, which is the price of the impetus. No new number enters the rules.
  • Ground. A charge whose path enters forest, swamp or shallows, or climbs, lands no +2: the discount stands and the charger is still exposed. A charge that starts from above, the charger's starting hex higher than the target's, puts the target's save against disorder at −2, whatever hex the charge ends on. The path is any path the movement allows; a straight-line rule does nothing on a two-hex charge and the approach already takes the cheapest way. Mark: "add the terrain line", and "What if we give the save penalty on a downhill charge?", then: "That would require that the charging unit start its charge from a hex that is at a higher elevation than the target." Cavalry through the woods is what every wargame disorders, and a hilltop had no melee value until now: attacking upward is −1 a level and a two-level edge is a cliff nobody fights across.
  • Reach: infantry charges 2 hexes, which is short range. A paced troop charges 4, into medium, and its extra action is movement, so it charges and Presses with an action to spare.
  • Cavalry charge (the tactic) is the impact: the charge's hit needs no save, so a Strike lands as a Press and a Press as an Overrun, at the price paid. The mounted signal goes; pace already carries the charge.
  • The counter is already on the board: Take cover holds the ground an Overrun would take, and a charge cannot end across a standing wall or a cliff, as today's Fight cannot.
  • Ancestors: Sudden Charge, two actions: "Stride twice. If you end your movement within melee reach of at least one enemy, you can make a melee Strike against that enemy." Shove is already Overrun's.
  • A charge may not begin in contact; that is a Fight. A unit under Pin is held and leaves its hex by Withdraw, so it cannot charge. Leftover charge movement does not bank. Fear on contact can rout the charger before the melee, and the charge then costs its movement alone, as the engine already plays it.

Grades become the extra action

Grades came in with push and outlived it: since push went, a grade decides nothing but the price of a rung. Mark: "It's a concept that came with pushing, and I don't think it fits any longer." Grades go. Every rung costs its own number of actions for every troop, Strike 1, Press 2, Overrun 3, and what a better troop gets is an extra action: four in the activation instead of three. Pathfinder's quickened is the ancestor: "You gain 1 additional action at the start of your turn each round. Many effects that make you quickened require you use this extra action only in certain ways. If you become quickened from multiple sources, you can use the extra action you've been granted for any single action allowed by any of the effects that made you quickened." So it is one action, never two, and each source names what it buys. The one-attack rule stands, so it never buys a second Strike.

  • The four reviewed ladders each lose their grade line at the build. Section 6's price table and its grade paragraph go with them, and a compelled unit's cap at its grade needs a new reading.

Free strike and no retreat

Unchanged. The free strike is a hit capped at one wound with the Fortitude save as usual, and a troop that gives no retreat follows a withdrawal for one free Move. Neither carries a rung.

Cast

The tradition table and the one-attack slot stand. What goes is the axis: a tier no longer buys one of range, duration or effect, and the +2 and +4 effect-roll bonuses go with it. The Cast-only actions go too: section 11 gave a caster level ÷ 5 actions that nothing but a cast could spend, paid for a cast that was a roll to cast and then a roll for the effect. A cast is now one roll, so a caster has the three actions every unit has, and a Burst is its activation. Every tree is a plain ladder of three cumulative tiers at the tree's own range, priced one, two and three actions. A buff lasts until the ally has next acted; an effect that lasts longer says so in its cell. A tree is cast once an activation; whether a caster casts once an activation altogether is open.

Blast · long range · the activation's attack

RungCostWhat it does

Burst◆◆◆A corner of the grid within range, the caster's choice, and the three hexes that meet at it. The one roll is read against the enemy in each: a hit 1 wound, a critical 2, then the save, as any hit.
  • Changed: Every bonus goes, and the range and duration pushes with them. A rung buys a shape in hexes: one, two on a line from the caster, three around a corner. Every hex of the shape is a full target. The lingering wound is gone.
  • Ancestors: Force Barrage and Blazing Bolt, one more target for each action spent. Lightning Bolt is the line, Fireball the burst. Both are read through the one spell attack, so there is one gate.
  • The number: at parity each hex the roll is read against adds 0.35 wounds expected, so a full Line expects 0.80 wounds and a full Burst 1.20, a Missile an action all the way up. A Burst read against every hex around the target expected 2.50 inside a block, six attacks' worth, and a save for the neighbours would have been a second gate.

Controlling · medium range · the target's Will save against your spell DC

One save gates every rung. On a critical success nothing happens. On a success the target is frightened, whatever the rung: −1 to everything it rolls and to its Defence until the end of its next activation, a light disorder that wears off. A failure is read off the rung, and a critical failure is the rung and 2 disorder in place of Dread's 1, whatever the rung.

RungCostWhat it does
Dread◆On a failed save the target takes 1 disorder; on a critical failure, 2.
Stun◆◆Dread, and on a failed save it has one action fewer on its next activation.
Hold◆◆◆Stun, and on a failed save it is rooted on its next activation: no Move, Charge or Withdraw.
  • Changed: Tier 1's −1 movement becomes the point of disorder. Tier 2 stands under Stun's name, since stunned wears off next round and slowed persists; it is the mirror of the extra action: a better unit gets one more, a stunned one has one fewer. Tier 3's "may not pay for a rung above its grade" is void, since grades are gone and nothing gates a rung but its price; rooted takes its place, which the game already has a word for. A success now bites, as every ancestor's does.
  • Ancestors: Fear is Frightened 1 on a success, 2 on a failure, 3 and fleeing on a critical failure. Paralyze is Stunned 1 on a success, paralyzed a round on a failure. Slow is left, with its minute-long duration. Rooted is the thing Guard already does to itself, applied from outside.
  • Frightened is a condition, so Healing's Restore can end it.
  • The demoralize tactic, which today grants the old Tier 3, grants Dread: that is what Demoralize does.
