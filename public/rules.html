Field Battles

Rules for resolving a battle between armies in a single sitting.

In one paragraph. Each army is a unit on a square of an eight-by-eight board. Units move like rooks, one square at a time. On its activation a unit takes three actions and keeps one reaction: advance, withdraw, strike, volley, brace or rally. Every roll is an ordinary d20 check using numbers already on the army's troop sheet. Hits deal wounds, one or two at a time; four wounds destroy a unit. Taking wounds forces morale checks; failing them makes a unit shaken, and a unit that is shaken three times routs. The battle ends when one side has nothing left standing, or at dusk after six rounds.

Quick reference
Worked battle

1. What you need

Each army's troop sheet, a d20, and a chessboard: eight files lettered a to h and eight ranks numbered 1 to 8, or the battle board in the kingdom app, which generates one from the hex. Each player commands the armies they lead and any the GM hands them. The GM commands every army of an enemy faction and acts for them in initiative order.

A battle happens when armies of hostile factions end up in the same hex. The side that moved in is the attacker; the side already there is the defender. The defender's hex sets the ground (section 9). The battle resolves inside the kingdom's Actions phase, before the next kingdom action.

2. The unit

An army in battle is a unit, and a unit is one of two types, the same two the skirmish rules use: infantry or cavalry. A siege engine is never a unit; it rides with one (section 10). You never invent new numbers for a unit; every value comes from its troop sheet.

3. The battlefield

The battlefield is a chessboard. Files run a to h across the board and ranks 1 to 8 from the attacker's edge to the defender's. Name a square by file and rank: c2, e7. Every unit stands on one square, and a square holds at most one unit.

Units move like rooks: one square along a file or a rank per Advance, never diagonally. The distance between two units is the count of orthogonal steps from one square to the other: from c5 to d7 is one file and two ranks, distance 3. Distance sets the range:

DistanceRangeStrikeVolley
1EngagedYesNo
2CloseNoAny Salvo
3LongNoSalvo with long or extreme reach
4 or moreExtremeNoSalvo with extreme reach, or a siege engine, at −2

Two units on orthogonally adjacent squares are engaged, unless a barrier lies on the edge between them (section 9). Diagonal neighbours stand at distance 2 and are never engaged. A unit engaged with two or more enemy units is outflanked and takes −2 to Defence.

Squares carry terrain and height; edges carry walls and cliffs. Eight files give room for a refused flank, a reserve rank and a walled corner.

Hex variant

The battle board can also generate as an eight-by-eight grid of hexes instead of squares, chosen when you generate the battlefield. Deployment still holds to ranks 1 to 3 and 6 to 8. Read every "orthogonal" above as "adjacent": hexes have no diagonal, so the rule that diagonal neighbours stand at distance 2 and are never engaged is a square-board rule only — on hex every neighbour is adjacent, at distance 1, and engaged if nothing stands on the edge between them. Every other number on this page — Volley bands, wounds, morale, wall and cliff rules — carries over unchanged, but the hex grid itself is roomier: eighteen cells fall within distance 2 of a given cell against a square board's twelve, so Volley and Demoralize reach further across the board in practice; the front deployment ranks meet at more points, so the opening clash is wider; and a unit that Withdraws while unengaged has two cells that count as homeward instead of one. These are properties of the grid, not a rule change made to fix them — whether the numbers above should move for hex play is still being tested.

4. Setting up

Setup runs in four stages, each on its own screen in the app.

Stage 1: generate the battlefield

Pick the hex's terrain (plains, forest, hills, mountains, swamp or desert), add a feature if the hex has one (a river or a lakeside) and a construction if it holds one (a fort of tier 0 to 3), then roll a seed. The generator lays out the board; the same seed always gives the same board. Reroll until the board looks like the hex. Hex types are tendencies: a plains board can carry a copse and a hills board a marsh, never many. A river always runs on ranks 4 and 5, and water never lands on a deployment rank, so both sides can always deploy.

Stage 2: paint

The GM adjusts the board by hand: set any square's terrain, set its height to 0, 1 or 2, put a wall on any edge, or clear a square. This is where a hazard from the adventure goes: the bridge, the burning barn, the ravine.

Stage 3: place units

Build each side from the troop library, the force generator or a custom card, attach siege engines to the units that bring them, then put every unit on a square. The attacker deploys anywhere on ranks 1 to 3, the defender anywhere on ranks 6 to 8, never on water. A unit with an ambush tactic may deploy one rank further in. Both sides need at least one unit and every unit needs a square. Note each unit's wounds and shaken from its sheet.

Two empty ranks lie between the lines. A unit on rank 3 reaches contact in its first activation; a unit held back on rank 1 takes two. The depth of your deployment is your first decision.

Every player commands a unit. When the players' side brings fewer armies than players, split armies before deployment: one army becomes two or more units of the same card, each with its own square, wounds and shaken. Each half is a full unit, and each is a separate target for outflanking and for rout checks; a player who commands one may lose it. After the battle the halves rejoin: wounds take the average rounded up, shaken takes the higher. The GM may also hand an allied faction's unit to a player for the battle.

The opposing force. The GM picks the enemy from the library or lets the generator build one. The generator reads the other side and answers it: the same number of units give or take one, every unit within three levels of the other side's average, and the total level within a tenth of the other side's. At most one unit brings a siege engine. An attacker facing walls brings one three times in four, and it may be a ram; anyone else brings artillery one time in five. The generated force is a starting point; add to it or remove from it.

Stage 4: begin

Each unit rolls initiative: d20 + Perception. Units act in this order every round for the whole battle. The first unit activates.

5. The round

A round is an hour of battle. In initiative order, each unit that is not destroyed or routed takes three actions, then the next unit acts. Each unit also has one reaction per round, refreshed at the start of its activation. When every unit has acted:

  1. Every unit that must make a rout check does so (section 8).
  2. If one side has no unit that is neither destroyed nor routed, the battle ends. If this was the sixth round, dusk ends it.

This is the action economy you already play: "Advance, Advance, Strike", "Volley, Volley, Brace", "Rally, Withdraw".

6. Actions

Each action costs one of the unit's three actions unless stated.

Advance
Only while not engaged. Move one square along a file or rank. A unit with Pace moves two squares instead when both squares are open or settlement and neither is uphill. You cannot cross a barrier, enter water, or enter an occupied square. Ending next to an enemy engages it.
Withdraw
Move one square along a file or rank, away from every enemy you are engaged with; a unit that is not engaged moves one square toward its own edge. If you are engaged when you start, this costs two actions and each enemy engaged with you may spend its reaction on a free Strike at you as you go (a success deals 1 wound; no critical).
Strike
Only while engaged. Roll d20 + Strike bonus against the target's Defence. Critical success: 2 wounds. Success: 1 wound. Failure: nothing. Critical failure: you are exposed, −2 Defence until your next activation. Takes the multiple attack penalty.
Volley
Only while not engaged, at a target within your Volley reach. Roll d20 + Volley bonus against the target's Defence; at extreme range take −2. Firing at a target that is engaged with your own ally is −4. Critical success 2 wounds, success 1. Takes the multiple attack penalty.
Brace
+2 to Defence until your next activation. While braced, you may spend your reaction on a free Strike at an enemy that Advances into a square next to you (success deals 1 wound; no critical).
Rally
Roll a Will save against your rout DC (section 8). Critical success: shaken −2. Success: shaken −1. Failure: nothing. Critical failure: shaken +1.
Retreat (two actions)
Withdraw twice. The first square draws free Strikes as usual if you were engaged; the second does not. A unit that Retreats past its own edge leaves the battle intact.

Multiple attack penalty. Your second Strike or Volley in an activation is at −5, your third at −10. Free Strikes from reactions ignore the penalty and never critical.

One reaction. The braced free Strike, the free Strike at a withdrawing enemy, and every reaction tactic spend the unit's one reaction for the round. A single elite unit cannot cut down every unit that touches it.

Bonuses do not stack. Brace, cover and fortification all give circumstance bonuses to Defence; use the highest.

7. Wounds

Damage is never rolled. A hit deals one wound, a critical hit two. Each unit holds up to four:

WoundsConditionEffect
0–1—Fights normally
2Weakened−2 to Strike and Volley; Volley reach shrinks by one band
3BrokenAs Weakened; cannot Advance; makes a rout check every round
4DestroyedRemoved from the battle

The level difference between attacker and target does the work that damage dice do elsewhere. A level-6 troop striking a level-2 troop hits on about 7 and above and criticals from 17; the reverse hits only from 17 and cannot critical without help.

A unit that enters battle already hurt starts with wounds: 0 if above three-quarters of its hit points, 1 if above half, 2 if above a quarter, 3 if it has any left. After the battle, wounds are written back the same way.

8. Morale

Every unit tracks shaken from 0 to 3. Each point is −1 to every check the unit rolls. At shaken 3 the unit is routed: on each of its activations it must Retreat, it cannot Strike or Volley, and it leaves the battle when it passes its own edge.

Rout check triggers

At the end of the round, a unit makes one rout check if any of these happened:

  • it took a wound this round;
  • it is Broken;
  • half or more of its side's starting units are now destroyed or routed (checked once, the round it first happens).

The rout DC

The level DC of the highest-level enemy unit within close range of the checking unit. If no enemy is within close range, use the highest-level enemy on the field. Add 2 if that enemy has a fear aura or frightful presence. Subtract 2 if the checking unit holds its own fortification or stands higher than every enemy it is engaged with; these two do not stack.

The check

Roll a Will save against the rout DC. Critical success: this unit makes no further rout checks this battle. Success: nothing. Failure: shaken +1. Critical failure: shaken +2.

9. Ground, height and barriers

Three layers shape the field. Terrain on a square changes checks; height on a square decides who has the advantage; barriers on edges decide where units can go. Each type has one job: forest hides, swamp slows, water blocks, height commands, walls hold.

Terrain

Every square is one of six types. Open is the default and does nothing.

TerrainEffect
Open—
ForestCover: the occupant gains +1 Defence against Volleys. Cancels Pace and blocks Cavalry Charge.
SwampSlows: an Advance into swamp costs two actions and moves one square only; Pace and Cavalry Charge never apply. A unit in swamp Strikes at −1.
ShallowsA ford or marsh edge: as swamp, and a unit in shallows cannot Withdraw or Retreat while engaged.
WaterImpassable. No unit enters; Volleys cross it.
SettlementCover, as forest; open ground for movement.

Height

Every square has a height of 0, 1 or 2. Count the difference between the attacker's square and the target's:

  • Striking uphill: −1 to Strike per level of difference.
  • Volleying downhill: the target's cover does not apply and your Volley reaches one band further. Volleying uphill: −1 per level.
  • Advancing uphill cancels Pace for that Advance and blocks Cavalry Charge.
  • A unit standing higher than every enemy it is engaged with treats its rout DC as 2 lower (section 8).
  • An edge with a difference of 2 is a cliff.

Barriers

A barrier sits on the edge between two squares. Terrain fills a square; a barrier is a line a unit must get over. Units on either side of a barrier are not engaged across it and cannot Advance, Withdraw or Retreat across it unless the barrier says so.

BarrierCrossEngaged acrossFire across
Wall (tier 0–3)No, until breachedYes. The assaulting unit Strikes at −2; the garrison Strikes normally and Volleys at +1 at any rangeYes
Cliff (height difference of 2)NoNoThe upper side Volleys at +1; the lower side cannot Volley the upper

A wall is made of segments, one per edge, and each segment has wound boxes equal to the fortification's tier plus one. A siege engine may Bombard a segment: a Volley against Defence 10 + tier + the defender's level, whose wounds go to the segment instead of a unit. When a segment's boxes are all marked it is breached and removed, and the two squares behave as neighbours again. Only engines damage walls. A tier-0 wall is a palisade or hedge with a single box, and one hit from any engine breaches it. Walls are repaired through fortification maintenance, never during a battle.

A fort is a block of settlement squares against the defender's edge, walled on its front and flanks with the segments its tier allows: four at tier 0, up to seven at tier 3. A short budget leaves a gate open on one flank. The garrison's Defence and save bonuses from the fortification are already on its sheet.

10. Siege engines

A siege engine travels with an army, sits in that army's square and acts on its activation. Firing it (a Volley or a Bombard) spends one of that army's three actions and uses the engine's launch DC minus 10 as its bonus. An engine reaches extreme range without the −2 penalty and cannot fire while its army is engaged. A ram cannot fire at units; it may only Bombard a wall segment on an edge of its own square, and it rolls at +2. When an army is destroyed or routed it abandons its engines where it stands, and any enemy unit that ends the battle on that square or next to it captures them.

11. Trained tactics

An army that has trained a tactic gains its battle action. Untrained armies use only the actions in section 6.

Reactive AttackReaction: a free Strike at an enemy that Advances into a square next to you, without Bracing.
Raise ShieldsYour Brace gives +3 instead of +2.
Shield BlockReaction, once per round: reduce one wound result by 1.
Defend AlliesOne action: an ally next to you is not outflanked until your next activation.

Passive tactics and doctrines that change a troop's Armour Class, saves or fear immunity apply on their own, because every roll reads the live sheet.

12. Ending the battle

The battle ends when one side has no unit still standing, or at the end of round six.

The winner

Units keep their wounds. The winning kingdom rolls a Warfare check. Critical success: heal 1 wound on every damaged unit and gain 1 Fame. Success: heal 1 wound on one unit. Failure: nothing. Critical failure: every unit becomes shaken +1.

The loser

Units keep their wounds and shaken. The losing kingdom rolls a Defense check. Critical success: one destroyed unit survives at 3 wounds. Success: nothing. Failure: +1 Unrest. Critical failure: +1d4 Unrest. Surviving units fall back one hex toward their nearest friendly settlement.

After dusk

If both sides still stand after six rounds, both withdraw to their edges, keep their wounds and shaken, and the hex remains contested.

Back on the kingdom map

Wounds become hit points (section 7). Shaken becomes the army's demoralized value, keeping whichever is higher; an army at demoralized 3 disbands at upkeep unless it is rallied or supported. Split armies rejoin (section 4). Destroyed units disband, and their engines are captured or destroyed. Tend Wounded heals as usual.

13. Fighting against the odds

There are no points. Four things decide an uneven fight:

  • Level. Two levels of difference is noticeable; five is decisive.
  • Numbers. Outflanking and repeated rout checks let three lesser units grind down one greater unit, at the cost of wounds on two of them. Numbers pay off only when they reach a second side of the enemy, and a ridge or river decides where that second side is.
  • Ground. Forest, high ground, shallows and walls favour the defender. A small garrison behind walls is a real obstacle.
  • Time. Six rounds and the half-side rout trigger reward a decisive attacker and punish a hesitant one.

A side whose total levels are less than half the enemy's should hold ground or refuse battle.

Worked battle

A forest hex: every square is forest at height 0, and there are no barriers. The attacker brings Heavy Cavalry (level 7, Defence 25, Strike +12, Will +14, Perception +14, Speed 40 so Pace) and Line Infantry (level 6, Defence 24, Strike +11, Volley +11 with long reach, Will +13, Perception +13). The defender has Kobold Warriors (level 3, Defence 18, Strike +7, Volley +7 with close reach, Will +9, Perception +9) and Troll Marauders (level 8, Defence 25, Strike +13, Will +11, Perception +15, Speed 30 so Pace). Forest cancels Pace for both mounted units, and every unit has cover against Volleys.

Initiative: infantry 25, trolls 22, cavalry 19, kobolds 14. Deployment: infantry c2, cavalry e2, kobolds d7, trolls e7. Both sides keep a rank in hand.

Round 1. The infantry finds the kobolds at distance 6 and Advances three times, c3, c4, c5; the kobolds are now at distance 3, long range, but no action is left. The trolls Advance e6, e5, putting the infantry at c5 at distance 2, and Brace. The cavalry Advances e3, e4 and stands next to the trolls: engaged. It Strikes: +12 against Defence 25 + 2 for Brace = 27; roll 16, total 28, a hit, trolls 1 wound. The kobolds Advance to d6, which puts the infantry at distance 2, close range, and Volley: +7 against 24 + 1 cover = 25; roll 19, total 26, a hit, infantry 1 wound. They Brace. End of round: the trolls were wounded and check against the engaged level-7 cavalry, DC 23: Will +11, roll 14, total 25, success. The infantry was wounded and checks against the level-8 trolls within close range, DC 24: Will +13, roll 8, total 21, failure, infantry shaken 1.

Round 2. The infantry (shaken 1, −1 to checks) Advances to c6, next to the kobolds at d6: engaged. It Strikes: +11 − 1 against 18 + 2 Brace = 20; roll 15, total 25, a hit, kobolds 1 wound. A second Strike at −5: +5 against 20; roll 17, total 22, a hit. Kobolds at 2 wounds: Weakened. The trolls Strike the cavalry: +13 against 25; roll 12, total 25, a hit, cavalry 1 wound. Second Strike: +8 against 25; roll 9, total 17, a miss. They Brace. The cavalry Strikes the trolls: +12 against 27; roll 16, total 28, a hit. Trolls at 2 wounds: Weakened. Second Strike: +7 against 27; roll 6, total 13, a miss. It Braces. The kobolds, Weakened and engaged, Withdraw to d7 for two actions; the infantry spends its reaction on a free Strike: +11 − 1 against 18; roll 11, total 21, a hit. Kobolds at 3 wounds: Broken. They Brace. End of round: the kobolds were wounded and are Broken, one check against the level-6 infantry within close range, DC 22: Will +9, roll 12, total 21, failure, kobolds shaken 1. The trolls check against DC 23: Will +11, roll 4, total 15, failure, trolls shaken 1. The cavalry checks against DC 24: Will +14, roll 7, total 21, failure, cavalry shaken 1.

Round 3. The infantry Advances to d6, next to the kobolds at d7, and Strikes: +10 against 18 + 2 = 20; roll 16, total 26, a hit. Kobolds at 4 wounds: destroyed. The infantry Advances again to e6, next to the trolls at e5. The trolls are now engaged from e4 and e6: outflanked. The trolls (Weakened, shaken 1, outflanked) Strike the cavalry: +13 − 2 − 1 against 25 + 2 Brace = 27; roll 12, total 22, a miss. Second Strike: +5 against 27; roll 15, total 20, a miss. They Rally: Will +11 − 1 against DC 23; roll 15, total 25, success, shaken back to 0. The cavalry (shaken 1) Strikes the trolls: +12 − 1 against 25 − 2 outflanked = 23; roll 12, total 23, a hit. Trolls at 3 wounds: Broken. Second Strike: +6 against 23; roll 15, total 21, a miss. It Braces. End of round: the trolls were wounded, are Broken, and half their side is destroyed: one check against DC 23. Will +11, roll 3, total 14, failure, trolls shaken 1.

Round 4. The infantry Strikes the trolls: +10 against 23; roll 14, total 24, a hit. Trolls at 4 wounds: destroyed. The defender has no unit standing. The attacker wins in four rounds.

Afterwards. The attacker's Warfare check succeeds and heals the cavalry to 0 wounds. The infantry keeps 1 wound (72 of 96 hit points) and both units keep shaken 1, written back as demoralized 1. The trolls and kobolds disband. The defender's Defense check fails: 1 Unrest.

The transcript shows the shape of a field battle. The four-rank gap gives the kobolds one volley on the approach and no more. The trolls stand in the open and are flanked because the infantry walks round the fallen kobolds. The multiple attack penalty makes the second Strike a coin flip. The last unit of a side goes down to a rout trigger it can no longer pass. On a hills or river hex the trolls would have held a ridge or a ford, and the fight would run two rounds longer.
