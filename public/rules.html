Field Battles

Rules for resolving a battle between armies in a single sitting.

In one paragraph. Each army is a unit on a hexagon of sixty-one hexes. The sides alternate: one unit activates and takes three actions, then the other side answers. A unit spends those actions on battlefield activities. Shoot, Fight, Guard and Rally each offer three, priced at one, two and three actions; Cast offers six trees of three (section 11); moving offers its own (section 7). Every activity costs the same for every unit, and nothing is rolled for it. Spare actions buy another activity. An attack is one roll: a hit deals one wound, a critical two, and four wounds destroy a unit. A wound costs the target disorder unless its Fortitude saves. A Fight that misses costs the attacker disorder unless its Will saves. Every unit has three morale pips. The third disorder point fills the track and routs the unit. The battle ends when one side has nothing standing, or at dusk after six rounds.

Quick reference

1. What you need

Each army's troop sheet, a d20, and the battle board in the kingdom app: a hexagon of sixty-one hexes, files lettered a to i and ranks numbered 1 to 9, generated from the hex being fought over. Each player commands the armies they lead and any the GM hands them. The GM commands every army of an enemy faction.

A battle happens when armies of hostile factions end up in the same hex. The side that moved in is the attacker; the side already there is the defender. The defender's hex sets the ground (section 10). The battle resolves inside the kingdom's Actions phase, before the next kingdom action.

2. The unit

An army in battle is a unit, and a unit is one of two types: infantry or cavalry. A siege engine is never a unit; it rides with one or holds a hex of its own (section 12). You never invent a number for a unit. Every value comes off the troop sheet, and a card with no sheet takes the level tables for its type.

Level DCs are the standard table: level 1 is DC 15, 3 is 18, 6 is 22, 8 is 24, 12 is 30.

Reflex is the stat that tells troops apart: it spreads 5.6 points within a level, where Armour Class spreads 3.2. Breaking contact therefore reads off Reflex. Perception is displayed, but no rule reads it: there is no initiative roll (section 5). Fortitude reads for one thing, the save against the disorder a wound would cost (section 8).

A card with no sheet takes the level tables for its type. Infantry: high AC, moderate attack, high Will, moderate Reflex, moderate Fortitude, one hex an action. Cavalry: high AC, high attack, moderate Will, high Reflex, moderate Fortitude, two hexes an action.

Three actions

Every unit has three actions in its activation, and every activity costs one, two or three of them (section 6). The price is the same for every unit, so a better troop is better by its numbers. Haste alone grants a fourth action (section 11).

An importer reads no retreat off the recurring action names on a statblock (section 7). Melee drill, shields, formation and magic ward recur too, and the engine reads none of them: every troop's activities cost the same. It reads mounted no longer either, since pace already carries the charge.

Armour Class and attack DC say nothing about a troop

Across all 162 published troops, Armour Class spreads 3.2 points within a level and attack DC 2.6 — at level 12 every troop shares one attack DC. Both are functions of level, so neither says anything about a troop's character. Will spreads 5.1 and Speed falls across seven bands, so those two are read numerically, and the recurring action names carry the rest.

Morale

Every unit has three morale pips. Each point of disorder fills one pip, and the third point causes rout. Will affects resistance to repulse and hostile morale magic, and the chance to Rally. Fortitude resists disorder from wounds. Capacity is the same for every unit; saving throws provide the distinction between troops.

3. The battlefield

The battlefield is a hexagon of sixty-one hexes, in rows of 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 8, 7, 6 and 5. Files run a to i and ranks 1 to 9, from the attacker's edge to the defender's; e5 is the centre. Name a hex by file and rank: c3, e7. Every unit stands on one hex, and a hex holds at most one unit.

Hexes have no diagonal. Every one of a hex's six neighbours is adjacent, at distance 1, and the distance between two units is the number of steps from one hex to the other. Distance sets the band a shot falls in:

A shot reaches any target the shooter can see. Each band beyond the weapon's own effective range (its Reach, section 2) costs −2 on the roll, and nearer bands cost nothing (section 8). A short-reach troop shooting into extreme is at −6.

Two enemy units on adjacent hexes are engaged, unless a cliff lies on the edge between them. A unit engaged with two or more enemies is outflanked and takes −2 to Defence. An engaged unit cannot stride away and cannot shoot; it fights, guards, rallies, casts, or maneuvers (section 7).

Hexes carry terrain and height; edges carry walls and cliffs (section 10).

4. Setting up

Setup runs in five stages, each on its own screen in the app.

Stage 1: the battlefield

Pick the hex's terrain (plains, forest, hills, mountains, swamp or desert), add a feature if the hex has one (a river or a lakeside) and a construction if it holds one (a fort of tier 0 to 3), then roll a seed. The generator lays the board out, and the same seed always gives the same board. Reroll until it looks like the hex.

Terrain is a tendency, not a rule: a plains board can carry a copse and a hills board a marsh, never many. Hills and desert raise a low ridge, mountains a high one with peaks. A river runs across ranks 4 to 6, the band neither side deploys on, and carries one or two fords; a lake hugs one flank with a marshy shore. Water never lands on a deployment rank, so both sides can always deploy.

Stage 2: paint

The GM adjusts the board by hand: set any hex's terrain, set its height to 0, 1 or 2, put a wall on any edge, or clear a hex. This is where a hazard from the adventure goes — the bridge, the burning barn, the ravine.

Stages 3 and 4: the two forces

Build each side from the troop library, the force generator or a custom card, attach siege engines to the units that bring them, then put every unit on a hex. The attacker deploys anywhere on ranks 1 to 3, the defender anywhere on ranks 7 to 9, never on water. A unit with an ambush tactic may deploy one rank further in. Both sides need at least one unit, and every unit needs a hex. Note each unit's wounds and disorder from its sheet.

Three ranks lie between the lines. A unit on rank 3 reaches contact in its first activation; a unit held back on rank 1 takes two. The depth of your deployment is your first decision.

Every player commands a unit. When the players' side brings fewer armies than players, split armies before deployment: one army becomes two or more units of the same card, each with its own hex, wounds and disorder. Each half is a full unit, a separate target for outflanking and a separate candidate for routing; a player who commands one may lose it. After the battle the halves rejoin: wounds take the average rounded up, disorder the higher. The GM may also hand an allied faction's unit to a player for the battle.

The opposing force. The GM picks the enemy from the library or lets the generator build one. The generator reads the other side and answers it: the same number of units give or take one, every unit within three levels of the other side's average, and the total level within a tenth of the other side's. At most one unit brings a siege engine. An attacker facing walls brings one three times in four, and it may be a ram; anyone else brings artillery one time in five. Add to the generated force or take from it.

Stage 5: begin

The attacker's side activates first. There is no initiative roll.

5. The round

A round is an hour of battle, and every unit acts once in it. The sides alternate: after each activation, the side with more units still to act goes next, and on a tie the side that did not act last. You choose which of your own units takes each of your turns.

An activation is three actions. A unit attacks once per activation — one Fight, one Shoot, or one Blast. Further actions buy other activities, never a second attack.

When every unit has acted, the round ends:

  1. Each side that has lost half its starting units to death or rout takes it once, that round only: every unit of that side still standing gains 1 disorder.
  2. An emplaced engine with only the enemy beside it changes hands. Every engine reloads.
  3. If either side has no unit still standing, the battle ends. If this was the sixth round, dusk ends it.

Alternating activation keeps a round readable. No side takes its whole army's worth of actions while the other watches, and a side that outnumbers the other spends its surplus at the end of the round.

6. Battlefield activities

A unit spends its three actions on battlefield activities. An activity is a named act priced at one, two or three actions, in Pathfinder's sense: it buys more than the same actions would buy one at a time. Five verbs offer activities — Shoot, Fight, Guard, Rally and Cast — and moving offers three more.

Within a verb, the two- and three-action activities do what the one-action activity does and more. The buffs of section 11 are the exception: three separate effects at three prices.

Where a unit stands filters its menu, which is therefore never long. In contact it is offered Fight and Guard; in the open, Shoot and Guard. A unit at one or two disorder retains its ordinary activities. A routed unit may only Move or Maneuver. Rally is otherwise always offered. The price

Every activity has the same base price for every unit: the one, two or three actions its diamonds show. Choose the activity, its targets and any extra commitment before resolving it.

Commitment

Fight, Charge, Shoot, Rally, Blast and Healing allow one extra action for +2, or two for +4 on the activity's roll. Controlling allows the same bonus to its spell DC instead. The bonus applies to this activity alone, including every target of its shared roll. It adds no wounds or targets by itself and does not improve a repulse save, a wound save or an Aegis check.

Commitment stacks with existing bonuses, including inspiration and a charge's +2. Spend only actions you have, up to two extra even under Haste. Guard, Maneuver, Move and the Offense, Defense and Movement buffs retain their own prices and effects. Tradition limits the activity available, not extra commitment; battlefield medicine and demoralize can concentrate their one-action activity too.

An activation can be a Strike at +4, a Strike at +2 followed by Brace, Press followed by Brace, or Strike, Brace and Steady. Commitment trades actions for reliability; the activity buys its special effect.

One attack an activation. Fight, Shoot and a Blast share one slot. A second attack broke the pacing, so spare actions buy other activities instead. Everything else may be repeated — Guard, Rally, the other spells, moving — and a Cast tree once an activation.

7. Moving and breaking away

Move

One Move action buys the unit's hexes (its Speed, section 2). Each hex costs from that budget by its terrain: 1 for open ground or a settlement, 2 for forest or shallows, 3 for swamp, and 1 more for each level of height climbed. Water cannot be entered. So a 1-hex-an-action infantry troop spends two actions to enter forest and three to enter swamp, and a 2-hex-an-action cavalry troop spends one and two.

Movement banks across the whole activation, so a 2-hex troop that steps one open hex keeps the leftover hex for its next step. A unit may not enter an occupied hex, cross a standing wall, or cross a cliff. A breach in a wall is an ordinary crossing.

A flier pays 1 a hex whatever the ground and crosses walls and cliffs. Flight buys no extra distance: how far a unit goes is still its Speed.

Charge

Every unit may charge. A charge is a Fight with a run folded into it: one Speed of movement at the ordinary terrain prices, ending in contact. It costs what the Fight costs, so every unit can buy the whole ladder. The Fight is at +2, and the charger is exposed until it next acts: −2 Defence, the same condition a critically failed Strike hands out. The charge spends the activation's attack. A unit already in contact fights instead of charging.

ActivityCostWhat it does
Charge◆One Speed of movement ending in contact, then a Strike at +2. You are exposed until you next act. A miss repulses like any Fight.
Charge and Press◆◆Charge, then Press instead of Strike: on a hit, the target rolls its Fortitude save twice and keeps the worse.
Charge and Overrun◆◆◆Charge, then Overrun: on a hit, the target rolls its Fortitude save twice and keeps the worse, and it drives them one hex straight back; you take their ground.
  • Ground. A charge whose path enters forest, swamp or shallows, or climbs, lands no +2. It keeps the movement discount, and the charger is still exposed. A charge that starts from a hex higher than the target's puts the target's save against disorder at −2, wherever it ends. The path is any the movement allows. A flier's charge reads the ground like any other charge: flight prices the hex and leaves it rough. Sure footing (section 11) makes it open ground and keeps the +2.
  • Reach. The run covers one Speed, so infantry charges from distance 2 and a paced troop from distance 3. Rough ground now stops a short run rather than costing it the +2: one Speed of infantry cannot pay a forest hex's price at all, so a charge that must cross forest is no charge.
  • Cavalry charge, the tactic, is the impact: the target rolls its Fortitude save twice and keeps the worse, so a Strike lands as a Press and a Press as an Overrun, at the price paid.
  • The first unit you engage. You must charge the first unit you engage: a route that would bring the charger into contact with any enemy but its target is closed to it, so the run turns aside from every hex another enemy holds. An enemy across a wall or a cliff holds none of them, since neither edge holds engagement (section 10). Where more than one hex touching the target is in reach, the charge comes in on one that keeps the +2 — the run costs the same one action however far it carries.
  • The counter. Take cover holds the ground an Overrun would take, and a charge cannot end across a standing wall or a cliff.
  • A unit under Pin is held and leaves its hex by Maneuver, so it cannot charge. Leftover charge movement does not bank.

Maneuver

Maneuver is the action for moving while engaged or pinned. Choose an unoccupied, adjacent hex you can enter. The destination determines the outcome: Reposition keeps you engaged, so you can work around an enemy, form a flank or occupy better terrain; Withdraw ends contact. Both outcomes use the same activities and checks. Every further move while engaged requires another Maneuver. Once clear, use Move.

A routed unit must Maneuver toward its own edge, including while engaged. A rooted unit or a unit with zero Speed cannot Maneuver. The three activities differ in who rolls: at Break off you roll; at Disengage and Fighting retreat each holder rolls instead.

Fighting retreat◆◆◆Disengage, and a holder that fails its roll takes 1 disorder as well, drawn out of its line.

The Maneuver check is one roll, however many enemies hold you: d20 + Reflex, less disorder, against the highest attack DC among them, a holder's Strike plus 10 or a pinning shooter's Volley plus 10 (section 8). The one roll is read for every holder. An enemy with no melee attack holds nobody.

A Maneuver that changes your hex ends a pin, including when you reposition in melee. Occupied hexes, water, walls and cliffs obey the usual movement restrictions. You can choose a destination beyond one hex when Break off could reach it on a critical success; another result moves you to the legal adjacent hex nearest that destination. A troop that gives no retreat follows only if the move breaks its contact: one free Move of its own Speed to re-establish contact, dealing no damage. A critical success prevents pursuit. Against Disengage or Fighting retreat it follows only if it passes its roll. A pinning shooter lands no free strike, and above Break off it rolls like any holder.

8. Wounds

Damage is never rolled. A hit deals one wound, a critical hit two, and each unit holds four. The level difference between attacker and target does the work damage dice do elsewhere. A level-6 troop striking a level-2 troop hits on about 7 and criticals from 17; the level-2 troop hits only from 17 and cannot critical without help.

A unit that enters battle already hurt starts with wounds: 0 above three-quarters of its hit points, 1 above half, 2 above a quarter, 3 with any left. After the battle they are written back the same way.

The attack

Every attack is one roll in one direction. A Fight is d20 + Strike against the target's Defence; a shot is d20 + Volley the same way; a Blast is the caster's spell attack. Critical success deals 2 wounds, success 1, failure nothing. Nobody strikes back: the target answers in its own activation.

Shooting

A shot reaches any target the shooter can see. Each band beyond the shooter's effective range costs −2 on the roll, and nearer bands cost nothing. A target engaged with one of your own costs a further −4, because the two are too close together to pick apart. Suppress and Pin buy what volume fire does to the troop under it. Nobody shoots while engaged, in either direction.

Guard

Guard lasts until the unit next acts. Brace buys +2 Defence, Dig in adds protection against critical hits, and Take cover raises Defence to +4 and holds the ground. Choose the activity at its listed price.

9. Disorder and rout

Morale is one number: disorder, from 0 to 3 for every unit. Each point is −1 to everything the unit rolls and −1 to its Defence. At one or two disorder, the unit retains its ordinary activities, subject to the usual restrictions for contact, range and available actions.

At three disorder the unit is routed: it may only Move or Maneuver, and it leaves the field when it reaches its own edge. Any maneuver it makes runs for that edge. Disorder caps at three. A routed unit relies on an ally's Rally, Inspire or Healing to recover. Clearing one point returns it to two disorder and restores its ordinary activities.

Morale dots and the retreat arrow

The three round dots below a unit record disorder. The four square boxes above them record wounds. Both tracks use the same convention: filling the last box or dot reaches the terminal state. The fourth wound destroys the unit; the third disorder point routs it. Every disorder point appears on the morale track.

All filled pips in a track share its current colour, on both the board and the army cards. Health squares progress from one yellow to two orange, three red and four grey at death. Morale circles progress from one orange to two red and three grey at rout. Red means one point from the limit; grey means the track is full. A dead or routed miniature loses its colour, while each pip track retains its own severity colour.

The flag turns grey and a retreat arrow appears when all three morale dots fill. The arrow points toward the unit's home edge — down toward rank 1 for an attacker, up toward rank 9 for a defender. It indicates rout and the direction of retreat; movement still happens during the unit's activation. Clearing the third dot restores the side's flag colour and removes the arrow.

Rally

Every unit below three disorder may Rally, in contact or in the open. A routed unit can recover through an ally's Rally, Inspire or Healing. At two disorder it regains its ordinary activities, including its own Rally.

Rally and a Healing cast (section 11) are the only things that clear disorder. Roll d20 + Will, less disorder, against the rout DC: the level DC of the highest-level enemy within short range, or, when no enemy is that close, of the highest-level enemy on the field. Rallying under a dragon's eye is harder than rallying beside a levy.

The roll says how much clears, and the activity says who else it reaches. One roll is read for every unit reached. On a success a unit with disorder clears 1, and a unit with none is inspired: +2 to its next roll.

Concentrated Rally. Extra commitment improves the shared Rally roll. Three actions buy Steady at +4 for yourself, Rally at +2 for yourself and one adjacent ally, or Inspire at the normal modifier for every friendly unit within 2. You may still choose several separate Steady attempts instead.

Inspired is +2 to the first roll the unit makes, of any kind: an attack, a save against disorder, a Maneuver check. It is a status bonus, so it does not stack with another status bonus on the same roll. It lapses when that roll is made or when the unit has acted, whichever comes first, and a second Rally on an inspired unit adds nothing. A unit carrying disorder cannot be inspired, so Rally steadies a disordered line and lifts a steady one. A unit with nothing to clear therefore still has use for the activity.

10. Ground, height and walls

Three layers shape the field. Terrain fills a hex and changes what checks cost; height decides who has the advantage; barriers sit on edges and decide where units may go. Forest hides, swamp slows, water blocks, height commands, walls bar the way.

Height

Every hex stands at height 0, 1 or 2. Count the difference between the two hexes:

  • Attacking upward, in melee or with a shot, costs −1 for each level of difference.
  • Shooting downward counts the range one band closer, so one band's −2 is not taken, and the target's forest cover does not apply.
  • Climbing costs 1 a level on top of the terrain.
  • An edge with a difference of 2 is a cliff: nobody crosses it, and nobody is engaged across it.

Walls

A wall sits on the edge between two hexes. Nobody crosses it until it is breached, and nobody is engaged across it: the enemy on the far side holds nothing, strikes nothing and is struck by nothing. Shots cross a wall freely, so the two shoot each other at a hex's distance, and neither withdraws to leave. A defender standing beside a wall of its own is garrisoned and shoots at +1. An attacker takes a walled hex by breaching the segment and coming through the gap.

Only the attacker attacks a wall, and each segment has wound boxes equal to its tier plus one. Fight a segment on an edge of your own hex, or bombard one within artillery range. The DC is 10 + the wall's tier + the level of the highest defender still standing; a hit takes one box, a critical two, and a segment with no boxes left is breached and gone. A ram rolls at +2 and is good for nothing else. Walls are repaired through fortification maintenance, never during a battle.

A fort is a block of settlement hexes against the defender's edge, walled on its front and flanks with the segments its tier allows: four edges at tier 0, up to seven at tier 3. A short budget leaves a gate open on one flank. Whatever the fortification gives the garrison in Defence and saves is already on its sheet.

11. Magic

A unit whose statblock names a spellcasting entry is a caster, and every caster belongs to one of magic's four traditions: arcane, divine, occult, primal. A tradition does not carry every kind of magic — see Tradition, below.

Casting

A caster carries two more numbers on its sheet, alongside Battle DC and Salvo DC: spell attack, what the caster rolls to attack with Blast and to heal, and spell DC, what a target must beat to resist Controlling. Neither is Will or the level-DC table doing double duty — they're the caster's own imported numbers, the same way Strike and Battle DC already are.

A cast is priced like every other activity: one, two or three actions for the activity you choose from a tree, at the tree's own range. A tree is cast once an activation. A caster has the three actions every unit has, and a Blast spends the activation's one attack. A buff falls on the caster as readily as on an ally, and it lasts until the unit it fell on has next acted. So a caster's own ward stands through the enemy's turn, while its own sure strike, wrath or sure footing must be spent in the rest of the activation it cast in. Haste alone lasts two activations, and a caster hasting itself takes the first of the two actions at once. Translocate is instant.

A cast is one roll. Blast is a spell attack against Defence, and its one roll is read against every hex its shape covers. Healing is a spell attack against the level DC of the unit healed, read against each unit reached. Controlling is the target's Will save against your spell DC. Offense and Movement have nothing to resist and roll nothing; Defense's Aegis is the enemy's Will save against your spell DC.

12. Siege engines

An engine is attached to a unit and rides with it, or emplaced on a hex of its own. An emplacement is worked by whichever friendly unit stands in or beside it, and it needs no crew of its own; leave it alone and it is abandoned where it stands.

A loaded artillery piece replaces its unit's shooting profile, effective range and all: the unit shoots with the engine's launch bonus, from the engine's own Reach rather than its own, at the activity's price. An engine's Reach may be extreme, which no troop's own Salvo ever is; that is what siege equipment is for. No engine may fire while its crew is engaged, because nobody shoots in contact. A ram fights walls at +2 and does nothing else. Each engine fires once a round, whoever works it; where two friendly units both stand beside an emplacement, the crew is the first in deployment order.

An emplaced engine never moves, so an emplaced ram can only ever batter a wall on an edge of the hex it deployed in. A ram is normally attached.

A unit that is destroyed or routs abandons its engines where it stands. An emplacement left with only the enemy beside it changes hands at the end of the round — the engine is taken by standing on it, not by winning a fight over it — and an abandoned engine beside an enemy still standing at the end of the battle is captured.

13. Ending the battle

The battle ends at the end of a round: when a side has no unit still standing, or after the sixth round, whichever comes first. A unit is standing when it is neither destroyed nor routed nor gone from the field; a unit at two disorder still counts as standing.

Dusk

If both sides still stand after six rounds, both withdraw, keep their wounds and disorder, and the hex stays contested.

The kingdom's checks

A battle ends at wounds, disorder, units destroyed and engines captured. What the kingdom then makes of that result — Fame, Unrest, recovery — is a kingdom action of its own.

Back on the kingdom map

Wounds become hit points on the same four thresholds (section 8). Disorder becomes the army's demoralized value, keeping whichever is higher; an army at demoralized 3 disbands at upkeep unless it is rallied or supported. Split armies rejoin (section 4). Destroyed units disband; a captured engine changes owner and an abandoned one is lost. The loser's surviving units fall back one hex toward their nearest friendly settlement.

14. Fighting against the odds

There are no points. Four things decide an uneven fight:

  • Level. Two levels of difference is noticeable; five is decisive. Level sets the DC of the saves made against you: a wound from a high-level troop is harder to shrug off, and a line it repulses is harder to steady.
  • Morale. Every troop routs at three disorder. Will determines how well it resists repulse and hostile morale magic and how readily it rallies. Fortitude determines how well it resists disorder from wounds. A troll has strong Fortitude and weak Will.
  • Numbers. Outflanking pays only when it reaches a second side of the enemy, and a ridge or a river decides where that second side is. Three lesser units grind down one greater one through disorder, not through wounds.
  • Ground and time. Forest, high ground and walls favour the defender. Six rounds and the half-army check reward a decisive attacker and punish a hesitant one.

A side whose total levels are less than half the enemy's should hold ground or refuse battle.
