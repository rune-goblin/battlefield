Field Battles

Rules for resolving a battle between armies in a single sitting. Play it in the browser.

In one paragraph. Each army is a unit on a hexagon of ninety-one hexes. The sides alternate: one unit activates and takes three actions, then the other side answers. A unit spends those actions on battlefield activities. Shoot, Fight, Guard and Rally each offer three, priced at one, two and three actions; Cast offers six trees of three (section 11); moving offers its own (section 7). Every activity costs the same for every unit, and nothing is rolled for it. Spare actions buy another activity. An attack is one roll: a hit deals 1 damage, a critical 2. Every unit starts with 4 Health and 3 Morale. Damage reduces Health; at 0 Health the unit is destroyed. Taking damage costs 1 Morale unless its Fortitude saves. A Fight that misses costs the attacker 1 Morale unless its Will saves. At 0 Morale the unit routs. The battle ends when one side has nothing standing, or at dusk after six rounds.

Quick reference

1. What you need

Each army's troop sheet, a d20, and the battle board in the kingdom app: a hexagon of ninety-one hexes, files lettered a to k and ranks numbered 1 to 11, generated from the hex being fought over. Each player commands the armies they lead and any the GM hands them. The GM commands every army of an enemy faction.

A battle happens when armies of hostile factions end up in the same hex. The side that moved in is the attacker; the side already there is the defender. The defender's hex sets the ground (section 10). The battle resolves inside the kingdom's Actions phase, before the next kingdom action.

2. The unit

An army in battle is a unit, and a unit is one of two types: infantry or cavalry. A siege engine is never a unit; it holds a hex of its own until a unit hauls it (section 12). You never invent a number for a unit. Every value comes off the troop sheet, and a card with no sheet takes the level tables for its type.

Level DCs are the standard table: level 1 is DC 15, 3 is 18, 6 is 22, 8 is 24, 12 is 30.

Reflex is the stat that tells troops apart: it spreads 5.6 points within a level, where Armour Class spreads 3.2. Breaking contact therefore reads off Reflex. Perception is displayed, but no rule reads it: there is no initiative roll (section 5). Fortitude reads for one thing, the save against the Morale loss damage would cause (section 8).

A card with no sheet takes the level tables for its type. Infantry: high AC, moderate attack, high Will, moderate Reflex, moderate Fortitude, one hex an action. Cavalry: high AC, high attack, moderate Will, high Reflex, moderate Fortitude, two hexes an action.

Three actions

Every unit has three actions in its activation, and every activity costs one, two or three of them (section 6). The price is the same for every unit, so a better troop is better by its numbers. Haste alone grants a fourth action (section 11).

An importer reads no retreat off the recurring action names on a statblock (section 7). Melee drill, shields, formation and magic ward recur too, and the engine reads none of them: every troop's activities cost the same. It reads mounted no longer either, since pace already carries the charge.

Armour Class and attack DC say nothing about a troop

Across all 162 published troops, Armour Class spreads 3.2 points within a level and attack DC 2.6 — at level 12 every troop shares one attack DC. Both are functions of level, so neither says anything about a troop's character. Will spreads 5.1 and Speed falls across seven bands, so those two are read numerically, and the recurring action names carry the rest.

Morale

Every unit has 3 Morale. Each missing point imposes −1 to rolls and Defence. At 0 Morale the unit routs. Will affects resistance to repulse and hostile Morale magic, and the chance to Rally. Fortitude resists Morale loss from damage. Capacity is the same for every unit; saving throws provide the distinction between troops.

3. The battlefield

The battlefield is a hexagon of ninety-one hexes, in rows of 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 10, 9, 8, 7 and 6. Files run a to k and ranks 1 to 11, from the attacker's edge to the defender's; f6 is the centre. The maximum distance is ten hexes. Setup also offers the original 61-hex board, and saved smaller battles retain their dimensions. Name a hex by file and rank: c3, e7. Every unit stands on one hex, and a hex holds at most one unit.

Hexes have no diagonal. Every one of a hex's six neighbours is adjacent, at distance 1, and the distance between two units is the number of steps from one hex to the other. Distance sets the band a shot falls in:

Reach is the weapon's preferred band. It shoots normally there and may flex one hex shorter or longer at −2. It cannot attack targets more than one hex outside its preferred distances, even with extra commitment or height. A long weapon prefers four hexes and allows three or five at −2. Extreme prefers five to seven and allows four or eight at −2. Spells retain fixed distance ceilings: Blast four hexes, Controlling three, and short-range buffs two.

Two enemy units on adjacent hexes are engaged, unless a cliff lies on the edge between them. A unit engaged with two or more enemies is outflanked and takes −2 to Defence. An engaged unit cannot stride away and cannot shoot; it fights, guards, rallies, casts, or maneuvers (section 7).

Hexes carry terrain and height; edges carry walls and cliffs (section 10).

4. Setting up

Setup runs in five stages, each on its own screen in the app.

Stage 1: the battlefield

Pick the hex's terrain (plains, forest, hills, mountains, swamp or desert), add a feature if the hex has one (a river or a lakeside) and a construction if it holds one (a fort of tier 0 to 3), then roll a seed. The generator lays the board out, and the same seed always gives the same board. Reroll until it looks like the hex.

Terrain is a tendency and the seed decides the rest: a plains board can carry a copse or a knoll, and a hills board a marsh. High ground takes one of several shapes — a spine from flank to flank, scattered knolls, heights on one flank, a central massif, or a pass between two shoulders — and stays off each side's two home ranks. Hills crown a few summits at height 2 with a slope on every side; mountains stand a third or more of their high ground at height 2 and break into cliffs, which may channel an advance and never seal it. Height runs from −2 to 2 on one scale, and sunken ground follows the rules of high ground: a step up costs 1 more, the higher unit holds the edge, and two levels between neighbours make a cliff. Every terrain may sink a hollow or two; a swamp board sinks a fifth or more of its ground, and mountains cut ravines to −2. Swamp and rough ground rarely share a board: when the seed draws both, nine boards in ten keep the commoner one. Woods favour the low ground, rough ground the slopes and swamp the hollows: the generator lays swamp at level 0 or below and water at level 0. A river runs across ranks 4 to 8, the band neither side deploys on, and carries one or two fords; a lake hugs one flank with a marshy shore. Water never lands on a deployment rank, so both sides can always deploy.

Stage 2: paint

The GM adjusts the board by hand: set any hex's terrain, set its height from −2 to 2, put a wall on any edge, or clear a hex. The generator keeps swamp at level 0 or below; the brush puts swamp at any height, for the upland bog the adventure calls for. This is where a hazard from the adventure goes — the bridge, the burning barn, the ravine.

Stages 3 and 4: the two forces

Place the siege engines first, then build each side from the troop library, the force generator or a custom card and put every unit on a hex. An engine stands on any dry hex of the board and belongs to neither army. The unit deployed on an engine claims it and works it from there; if the engine can move, the player chooses whether the unit starts the battle hauling it. An engine with no unit on it goes to the army with a unit deployed beside it. No other two pieces share a hex. The attacker's units deploy anywhere on ranks 1 to 3, the defender's anywhere on ranks 9 to 11, never on water. A unit with an ambush tactic may deploy one rank further in. Both sides need at least one unit, and every unit needs a hex. An engine left off the board stays out of the battle. Note each unit's Health and Morale from its sheet.

Five ranks lie between the deployment zones. Against a stationary opponent on rank 9, a Speed-1 unit on rank 3 can attack in its second activation over open ground. Both armies advancing meet sooner. The depth of your deployment is your first decision.

Every player commands a unit. When the players’ side brings fewer armies than players, split armies before deployment: one army becomes two or more units of the same card, each with its own hex, Health and Morale. Each half is a full unit, a separate target for outflanking and a separate candidate for routing. After the battle the halves rejoin: Health takes the average rounded down, and Morale takes the lowest value. The GM may also hand an allied faction’s unit to a player for the battle.

The opposing force. The GM picks the enemy from the library or lets the generator build one. The generator reads the other side and answers it: the same number of units give or take one, every unit within three levels of the other side's average, and the total level within a tenth of the other side's. The generator builds units alone; siege engines are placed by hand. Add to the generated force or take from it.

Stage 5: begin

The attacker's side activates first. There is no initiative roll.

5. The round

A round is an hour of battle, and every unit acts once in it. The sides alternate: after each activation, the side with more units still to act goes next, and on a tie the side that did not act last. You choose which of your own units takes each of your turns.

An activation is three actions. A unit attacks once per activation — one Fight, one Shoot, or one Blast. Further actions buy other activities, never a second attack.

When every unit has acted, the round ends:

  1. An emplaced engine with only the enemy beside it changes hands, and an unclaimed engine goes to the army that alone stands by it. Every engine reloads.
  2. If either side has no unit still standing, the battle ends. At the daily round limit, dusk ends the day. The default is six rounds; setup also offers eight.

Alternating activation keeps a round readable. No side takes its whole army's worth of actions while the other watches, and a side that outnumbers the other spends its surplus at the end of the round.

6. Battlefield activities

A unit spends its three actions on battlefield activities. An activity is a named act priced at one, two or three actions, in Pathfinder's sense: it buys more than the same actions would buy one at a time. Five verbs offer activities — Shoot, Fight, Guard, Rally and Cast — and moving offers three more.

Within a verb, the two- and three-action activities do what the one-action activity does and more. The buffs of section 11 are the exception: three separate effects at three prices.

The price

Every activity has the same base price for every unit: the one, two or three actions its diamonds show. Choose the activity, its targets and any extra commitment before resolving it.

Commitment

Fight, Charge, Shoot, Rally, Blast and Healing allow one extra action for +2, or two for +4 on the activity's roll. Controlling allows the same bonus to its spell DC instead. The bonus applies to this activity alone, including every target of its shared roll. It adds no damage or targets by itself and does not improve a repulse save, a Morale save or an Aegis check.

Commitment stacks with existing bonuses, including inspiration and a charge's +2. Spend only actions you have, up to two extra even under Haste. A spell’s total commitment is at most three actions. Guard, Maneuver, Move and the Offense, Defense and Movement buffs retain their own prices and effects. Tradition limits the activity available, not extra commitment; battlefield medicine and demoralize can concentrate their one-action activity too.

An activation can be a Strike at +4, a Strike at +2 followed by Brace, Press followed by Brace, or Strike, Brace and Steady. Commitment trades actions for reliability; the activity buys its special effect.

One attack an activation. Fight, Shoot and a Blast share one slot. A second attack broke the pacing, so spare actions buy other activities instead. Everything else may be repeated — Guard, Rally, the other spells, moving — and a Cast tree once an activation.

7. Moving and breaking away

Move

One Move action buys the unit's hexes (its Speed, section 2). Each hex costs from that budget by its terrain: 1 for open ground, a settlement or a bridge, 2 for forest, rough ground or shallows, 3 for swamp. A step up a level costs 2. Costs never add: a hex costs the worst of its terrain, its climb and a siege field, so a climb into forest costs 2 and a climb into swamp 3. Water cannot be entered. So a 1-hex-an-action infantry troop spends two actions to enter forest and three to enter swamp, and a 2-hex-an-action cavalry troop spends one and two.

An enemy controls each adjacent hex across a traversable edge. A Move may enter that zone but ends at its first contact hex. Moving through or out of contact requires Maneuver and its rolls (below). Splitting a route into several Moves cannot bypass this rule. Press Space while dragging to pin a waypoint on the hex under the pointer, and again on the same hex to lift it; the route then runs through each waypoint in order and costs the whole road. A waypoint must lie within reach and outside every zone of control, except the last, where the route ends. A Charge, and the move before one, honour waypoints the same way. Maneuver and Flee ignore them. Walls and cliffs interrupt control. A Charge ends at its first contact with its target and cannot pass through another enemy's zone. A critical Maneuver can clear the holders it checks, but ends when it reaches a new enemy's zone.

Maneuver also checks terrain. Its adjacent step must fit one Speed of movement per action spent on the activity. A slow unit needs at least two actions to maneuver into forest and three into swamp, and two to step up a level onto open ground. A critical Break off can follow a longer route within one Speed, ending at any new enemy contact.

Movement banks across the whole activation, so a 2-hex troop that steps one open hex keeps the leftover hex for its next step. A unit may not enter an occupied hex, cross a standing wall, or cross a cliff. A breach in a wall is an ordinary crossing.

A flier pays 1 a hex whatever the ground and crosses walls and cliffs. Flight buys no extra distance: how far a unit goes is still its Speed.

Flee the battlefield

A unit may leave through any outer map edge in its own deployment zone, including the side edges and an ambusher’s extra deployment rank. Drag the unit to a boundary hex or just beyond its outer edge, choose Flee, and confirm the route and total cost. Pay the ordinary movement cost to reach that hex, then one action to flee. Rooted or pinned units must clear the restriction first. A unit in contact must Maneuver to reach the edge; one already on the edge may flee directly.

Roll the unit’s Will with its current modifiers against its Rally DC, measured from the exit hex. The unit leaves the field on every result, and the exit provokes no free strike. Success or critical success sends it to camp with its current Morale and Health unchanged. It takes no further turn that day, remains a survivor in the report, and may recover and redeploy for another day. Failure or critical failure reduces its Morale to 0: it escapes routed, abandons attached equipment, and cannot recover or return the next day. A unit already routed stays routed when it leaves. Units in camp do not hold the field; if the remaining force gives up the field, the opponent wins.

Charge

Every unit may charge. A charge is a Fight with a run folded into it: one Speed of movement at the ordinary terrain prices, ending in contact. It costs what the Fight costs, so every unit can buy the whole ladder. The Fight is at +2, and the charger is exposed until it next acts: −2 Defence, the same condition a critically failed Strike hands out. The charge spends the activation's attack. A unit already in contact fights instead of charging.

Maneuver

Maneuver is the action for moving while engaged or pinned. Choose an unoccupied, adjacent hex you can enter. The destination determines the outcome: Reposition keeps you engaged, so you can work around an enemy, form a flank or occupy better terrain; Withdraw ends contact. Both outcomes use the same activities and checks. Every further move while engaged requires another Maneuver. Once clear, use Move.

A routed unit must Maneuver toward its own edge, including while engaged. A rooted unit or a unit with zero Speed cannot Maneuver. The three activities differ in who rolls: at Break off you roll; at Disengage and Fighting retreat each holder rolls instead.

Fighting retreat◆◆◆Disengage, and a holder that fails its roll loses 1 Morale as well, drawn out of its line.

The Maneuver check is one roll, however many enemies hold you: d20 + Reflex, minus missing Morale, against the highest attack DC among them, a holder's Strike plus 10 or a pinning shooter's Volley plus 10 (section 8). The one roll is read for every holder. An enemy with no melee attack holds nobody.

A troop that gives no retreat follows only if the move breaks its contact: one free Move of its own Speed to re-establish contact, dealing no damage. A critical success prevents pursuit. Against Disengage or Fighting retreat it follows only if it passes its roll. A pinning shooter lands no free strike, and above Break off it rolls like any holder.

8. Health

Health measures survival. A unit at 1, 2 or 3 Health retains its ordinary attacks, saves, Defence, movement and range. At 0 Health it is destroyed. Taking damage still triggers a Fortitude save against Morale loss; each missing point of Morale imposes its own penalty.

Damage is never rolled. A hit deals 1 damage, a critical hit 2. Each point of damage removes 1 Health. Every unit has a maximum of 4 Health. The level difference between attacker and target does the work damage dice do elsewhere. A level-6 troop striking a level-2 troop hits on about 7 and criticals from 17; the level-2 troop hits only from 17 and cannot critical without help.

A unit that enters battle already hurt starts with reduced Health: 4 above three-quarters of its hit points, 3 above half, 2 above a quarter, 1 with any hit points left, and 0 with none. After the battle, Health 4/3/2/1/0 becomes full/three-quarters/half/one-quarter/zero hit points, rounding fractions down.

The attack

Every attack is one roll in one direction. A Fight is d20 + Strike against the target's Defence; a shot is d20 + Volley the same way; a Blast is the caster's spell attack. Critical success deals 2 damage, success 1, failure nothing. Nobody strikes back: the target answers in its own activation.

Guard

Guard lasts until the unit next acts. Brace buys +2 Defence, Dig in adds protection against critical hits, and Take cover raises Defence to +4 and holds the ground. Choose the activity at its listed price. In shallows or swamp, Guard buys Brace alone.

9. Morale and rout

Morale runs from 3 to 0. Each missing point imposes −1 to every roll and to Defence. Missing Morale is 3 minus current Morale. At 3 Morale there is no penalty; at 2 Morale the penalty is −1; at 1 Morale it is −2. A unit with at least 1 Morale retains its ordinary activities, subject to contact, range and available actions.

At 0 Morale the unit is routed: it may only Move or Maneuver, and it leaves the field when it reaches its own edge. Any maneuver it makes runs for that edge. Its rolls and Defence take −3. Morale stays between 0 and 3. A routed unit relies on an ally’s Rally, Inspire or Healing to recover. Restoring 1 Morale ends the rout and restores its ordinary activities.

Health and Morale bars

Two bars beneath each unit show its remaining capacity. Both have the same length: the thicker upper bar shows Health out of 4, and the thinner lower bar shows Morale out of 3. Fine separators mark each point. Full green bars mean healthy and steady. Losses shrink the bars from right to left, leaving a subdued grey track. At 0 Health the unit is destroyed; at 0 Morale it routs.

Hover over a unit or its army card to read its remaining values, such as “Health 2/4” and “Morale 1/3”. Recovery refills the bars up to their maximums. A routed miniature loses its colour; each bar keeps its own severity colour. A destroyed unit leaves the board: a skull shows over it with the word “Destroyed”, then settles onto the ground of its hex, small, grey and faint. The mark stays for the battle, blocks nothing, and cannot be selected. Hover over the hex to read “unit name deceased”.

Rally

Every unit with at least 1 Morale may Rally, in contact or in the open. A routed unit can recover through an ally’s Rally, Inspire or Healing. At 1 Morale it regains its ordinary activities, including its own Rally.

Rally and a Healing cast (section 11) restore Morale during combat. Overnight Rally also restores Morale (section 13). Roll d20 + Will, minus missing Morale, against the rout DC: the level DC of the highest-level enemy within short range, or, when no enemy is that close, of the highest-level enemy on the field. Rallying under a dragon’s eye is harder than rallying beside a levy.

10. Ground, height and walls

Three layers shape the field. Terrain fills a hex and changes what checks cost; height decides who has the advantage; barriers sit on edges and decide where units may go. Forest hides, swamp slows, water blocks, height commands, walls bar the way.

This is advisory: the GM may keep the map, reroll it, or paint bridges or shallows to connect the banks. A wide river can require several adjacent crossing hexes. Terrain grants circumstance modifiers. Use the highest circumstance bonus and the worst circumstance penalty on each statistic. Mountain defence, forest cover and Guard bonuses never add together. Swamp does not add another Defence penalty to exposed or outflanked, and a unit attacking uphill from a ford takes −1 once. The penalty for missing Morale and other conditions retain their separate effects.

Sight and screening

Trace a straight line between hex centres. An intervening hex blocks shooting and spells when it stands at least as high as the higher of the two units and higher than the lower. So a hill hides the ground behind it from a unit on the flat, a unit on the hill sees both sides, and a mountain blocks every line across it from lower ground: you may target the mountain hex or shoot out of it, and a second mountain blocks the line to the ground beyond. Two units on one height see each other across a hex of that height. Forest along the line grants +1 Defence to the target against shooting and Blast, even when the attacker is higher, and two forest hexes along the line block it. The attacker's own forest hex does not screen its outgoing attacks. Forest does not improve Will saves against Controlling.

A line along a shared edge reads both hexes; touching a corner alone does not screen it. Every hex in a Blast Line or Burst must have sight from the caster. Flight changes movement, not sight. Walls retain their movement and garrison rules; they do not screen intervening shots.

Casting

  • Every unit takes three actions on its activation. Spell costs remain one, two, or three actions. Tier IV spells cost three actions.
  • Each caster may cast once from each tree per activation. Blast uses the same single attack allowance as Fight and Shoot. Casting uses actions and positioning; it requires no spell slots or mana.
  • Blast and Healing may spend extra actions for +2 on their roll per action. Controlling gains +2 spell DC per extra action. Total commitment is at most three actions. Tier never changes that price or bonus.
  • A level-5 arcane apprentice may spend three actions on Missile at +4. A level-10 arcane adept may spend three actions on Line at +2. A level-11 arcane caster may spend three actions on its newly available Soothe at +4. Extra commitment improves the available spell’s roll; its target count and shape stay fixed.
  • Each selected target must meet the tree’s range and ordinary line-of-sight rules. A caster may receive its own beneficial magic. A multi-target spell affects each recipient once. Choose all targets before rolling.
  • Beneficial spells affect surviving units, including undead troops and units that have routed. Restoring at least 1 Morale ends a rout. Destroyed units remain destroyed.
  • Spell buffs follow these stacking rules. Recasting the same active effect grants no additional benefit. Different effects can coexist. Ward and Sure strike cancel on the same attack. Sanctuary counts as Stoneskin, and Battle chorus counts as Sure strike for these rules.

Except for Haste and instantaneous transfers, a self-cast buff expires at the end of the caster’s current activation; a buff on another unit expires at the end of that unit’s next activation. Ward, Stoneskin, Aegis, and Sanctuary instead protect a self-caster through the end of its next activation. Each recipient tracks its own expiry.

Battlefield medicine continues to grant Soothe through Will. Demoralize continues to grant Dread through the troop’s level DC. Troop level alone grants these tactics no further spells.

12. Siege engines

An engine deploys on any dry hex, and the unit deployed on or beside it claims it for its army. An engine no unit claims at deployment belongs to neither army. It does nothing and stays where it stands until it is claimed: at the end of a round it goes to the army that alone has a unit standing on or beside it, and it waits while both armies do. Once claimed it is worked like any other emplacement, and hauled if it is mobile. A unit deployed on it may begin the battle hauling it, and an engine travels with a unit only while that unit hauls it. An emplacement is worked by whichever friendly unit stands in or beside it, and it needs no crew of its own; leave it alone and it is abandoned where it stands.

Stand in an engine’s hex to open its siege menu through the action wheel or floating badge. An engine fills an empty hex and becomes a badge when a unit occupies that hex. A friendly unit beside an engine reserves it; operating the engine requires occupation. Ordinary Shoot uses the troop's own weapon.

Attack offers activities from that engine's source actor. A catapult offers Bombard (one action, three hexes around a corner, 1/2 damage), Crushing shot (two actions, one unit, 2/3 damage) and Breach wall (two actions, 2/3 structural damage, penetration 1). Heavy artillery trades loading and attack actions for larger areas or heavier hits. Rams attack adjacent walls with their own launch bonus and penetration.

Each engine attacks once per round, and operating it spends the unit's attack for that activation. Artillery cannot fire in enemy contact. Roll the engine's launch bonus, minus the crew’s missing Morale, plus the crew's roll modifiers and any extra commitment. Roll against each affected unit's Defence. An area attack hits allies as well as enemies; the preview lists them. Damage caps from Dig in and Stoneskin still apply. Damage triggers a Fortitude save against the engine's level DC.

Siege Reach is a fixed ceiling: short 3 hexes, medium 5, long 7 and extreme 11. The whole selected area must fit the range and sight limit. High-angle activities ignore cover but still require sight. Trebuchets, mortars and other lobbers show their minimum range in the activity detail. Lines extend up to three hexes from the engine; fans cover three hexes; wide bursts cover a hex and its neighbours.

Artillery starts loaded. Load readies the next shot in one activity. A source loading cycle of up to four actions becomes one action, five to eight becomes two, and larger cycles become three. Heavy engines may reload in one activation and fire in the next. Rams and the Bolt Emitter need no reload. Loading cannot happen in enemy contact. Full-rack and overcharged attacks pay their extra preparation in the attack's action cost.

Special activities can leave persistent damage, hold movement through the target's next activation, remove an action from that activation, expose targets, push or pull a unit one clear hex, reduce Morale, or remove magical buffs. Forced movement respects barriers, occupied hexes, roots, Take cover and hauling. Marking powder causes exposure without damage. Pheromones affect cavalry, aquatic shocks affect units in water or shallows, and ground quakes affect ground troops.

Debris and web fields cost at least two hexes of movement to enter and stop charges through the end of the following round. Webs also let infantry without an engine in tow cross a wall between two webbed hexes. Fire clears webs in its affected area. A unit that takes a hit from webs loses movement through its next activation. Fields display their expiry round and clear overnight.

Haul siege engine costs one action. The engine travels with its unit, which moves at the slower of its own Speed and the engine’s Speed. Engine movement uses the same scale as troop movement: thirty sheet feet equal one battlefield hex. Round engine speed up to the nearest half hex; engines with sheet Speed 5–15 feet move half a hex per action, and those with Speed 20–30 feet move one hex. Portable engines use the crew’s Speed. Starting to haul clears banked movement, and a unit hauls one engine at a time. Release siege engine is free: it leaves the engine in the current hex and restores the unit’s normal movement rate. Moving without hauling leaves the engine behind. Mounted engines without a movement speed, and engines confined to tracks, remain fixed.

A unit that is destroyed or routs abandons its engines where it stands. An emplacement left with only the enemy beside it changes hands at the end of the round — the engine is taken by standing on it, not by winning a fight over it — and an abandoned engine beside an enemy still standing at the end of the battle is captured.

13. Dusk and the next day

The battle ends at the end of a round when a side has no unit still standing. A unit is standing when it is neither destroyed nor routed nor gone from the field; a unit at 1 Morale still counts as standing. Otherwise, dusk ends the day at the daily round limit: six rounds by default, or eight in setup.

The day's report

At dusk, resolve any pending persistent damage once before counting survivors. Both armies withdraw from contact and the hex stays contested. The report gives each army its own column with Health, Morale and unit outcomes. End battle returns to setup. Continue to recovery opens the recovery table before either army chooses its orders. After recovery, both armies choose whether to leave or remain. If both hold, Fight another day opens a battlefield choice, then deployment for the next day. Choose Same map to keep the current field or New map to generate and preview fresh terrain. The new map offers terrain, feature and fortification choices. Continuation requires both sides to have standing survivors.

Destroyed units are permanently lost. Routed units leave this battle and take no part in overnight recovery or the next day's deployment. Keep their records for the campaign handoff. A unit that rallied before leaving the field and remains standing can continue.

Overnight recovery

Resolve recovery before choosing to withdraw or hold the field. The recovery step gives each army its own column. For each survivor choose None, ⚑ Morale (Rally), or ♥ Health (Treat Wounded); the column’s foot shows that army’s shared recovery modifier. Roll recovery commits that army’s choices and rolls its units one after another, each shown in turn. The two armies roll independently, in either order, and an army that has rolled cannot change its choices. Review all results before choosing orders.

Each surviving unit may attempt one activity: Rally or Treat Wounded. Each side chooses any number of its survivors and declares them all before it rolls. The first participant adds no penalty; each extra participant imposes −2 on every recovery check for that side. Count both activities together: one participant gives 0, two give −2, three give −4, and so on. The opposing side counts its participants separately.

Both failure and critical failure leave the unit unchanged. Recovery stops at full Health or Morale. Rally changes Morale alone; Treat Wounded restores Health alone. A unit with full Morale cannot choose Rally, and a unit at full Health cannot choose Treat Wounded. Neither activity requires a leader or a character skill check.

Use d20 + the unit’s save − missing Morale − 2 × (its side’s participants − 1). Missing Morale is 3 minus current Morale. The campaign’s Demoralized condition supplies that same penalty; apply it once. For example, a unit with Will +12 and 1 Morale rolls at +10 alone, +8 with one other participant, or +6 with two others. Resolve each declared check once; its result is final for that night.

End-of-day decisions

After overnight recovery at a contested dusk, each army chooses Propose surrender, Withdraw, or Hold the field. Confirm both decisions before choosing the next battlefield. Recovery results stand even if an army then withdraws.

  • Propose surrender. The opposing side accepts or rejects the proposal. Acceptance ends the battle with the accepting side victorious. The players agree the campaign terms; the game preserves surviving units, Health and Morale. Rejection returns the proposing side to its decision. A proposal alone never ends the battle.
  • Withdraw. Leave the field with surviving troops, preserving Health and Morale. At dusk this requires no check and causes no extra casualties. If the opponent holds, they win the field. If both withdraw, the field stays contested and this battle ends.
  • Hold the field. Stay to contest the ground. If both sides hold, continue through the battlefield choice and deployment. If the enemy withdraws, hold the field as victor.

Resolve any surrender proposal before confirming the other decisions. Recovery occurs once, before these decisions; changing an order grants no further recovery.

The next day's deployment

Keep Health and Morale after recovery. Temporary combat conditions and spell effects expire overnight; actions and engine shots reset. On the same map, breached walls remain breached and emplacements keep their positions and ownership. On a new map, terrain and fortifications come from the new map settings. Fixed emplacements and abandoned equipment stay on the old field; the battle record retains that field and its equipment for the campaign handoff. Equipment losses remain. Surviving units redeploy in their normal home zones on the chosen board; attached engines still in their crews' possession move with them. Changing the map after recovery preserves the recovery results. The next day begins at round 1. The log retains the earlier days and recovery rolls.

Campaign Morale and Routed armies

Campaign Demoralized maps to missing Morale. Starting Morale is 3 minus the campaign army’s Demoralized value, bounded between 0 and 3. Battle losses, combat rallies and overnight rallies change that same track. Export Demoralized as 3 minus final Morale, including improvements from recovery. Each missing point gives −1 to checks and Defence; apply the penalty once.

In the ReignMaker integration, an army that leaves the battle through rout retains the Routed campaign condition. Rally Troops costs one leader action and makes a leadership skill check at −5. Success removes Routed and sets Demoralized to 2 (1/3 Morale), preserving Health. Failure leaves Routed in place. Every army still Routed at the end of the ReignMaker turn disbands. Disbanded and destroyed armies are permanent losses. This paid action replaces the proposed free post-battle retention check.

Rally Troops and turn-end disbanding belong to ReignMaker; Battlefield provides the tactical outcome. A battlefield day does not itself advance the ReignMaker turn. Health becomes hit points on the same four thresholds (section 8). Split armies rejoin (section 4). A captured engine changes owner and an abandoned one is lost. The loser's surviving units fall back one hex toward their nearest friendly settlement. Fame, Unrest and other kingdom consequences remain campaign actions.

14. Fighting against the odds

There are no points. Four things decide an uneven fight:

  • Level. Two levels of difference is noticeable; five is decisive. Level sets the DC of the saves made against you: damage from a high-level troop is harder to shrug off, and a line it repulses is harder to steady.
  • Morale. Every troop routs at 0 Morale. Will determines how well it resists repulse and hostile Morale magic and how readily it rallies. Fortitude determines how well it resists Morale loss from damage. A troll has strong Fortitude and weak Will.
  • Numbers. Outflanking pays only when it reaches a second side of the enemy, and a ridge or a river decides where that second side is. Three lesser units grind down one greater one by breaking its Morale.
  • Ground and time. Forest, high ground and walls favour the defender. The daily round limit rewards a decisive attacker.

A side whose total levels are less than half the enemy's should hold ground or refuse battle.
