Equal progression. Every tradition follows the same schedule: three starting trees, two secondary trees at level 6, and one weak tree at level 11. Each signature or strong tree advances one spell tier at levels 6 and 11; the two signature trees reach IV at level 16. Total repertoire grows from 3 → 8 → 14 → 16 spells.

Shared benefits and constraints. Every tradition has the same number of spells at each spell tier within each level band: three tier I spells initially; five tier I and three tier II spells at level 6; six tier I, five tier II, and three tier III spells at level 11; then two tier IV spells at level 16. Every spell keeps its listed action cost, range, roll, and concentration rules across traditions. Delayed access changes the learning level. It grants the spell at its original tier and cost.

Mastery distribution. Every tree reaches tier IV in at least one tradition. Arcane and Primal share Blast mastery; Divine and Primal share Healing mastery. Occult alone masters Controlling and Offense, Divine alone masters Defense, and Arcane alone masters Movement. Each tradition has a distinct pair of masteries. Equal access counts give the draft a common starting point for balance; spell effects and combinations require playtesting.

Design review

Four tiers fit the game. Three unlocks across twenty levels give the player a short progression to remember. The shared ReignMaker bands reinforce it. The catalogue grows from eighteen effects to twenty-four, and tradition ceilings keep an individual caster’s menu smaller. The main complexity comes from spell effects and targeting, rather than the fourth tier itself.

Earlier spells keep a purpose. Tiers I and II offer cheaper casts and concentration. Tier III offers single-target control, haste, attack prevention, and greater transfer distance. Tier IV expands coverage or deepens recovery. An archmage still chooses between spells according to the position on the board.

Pathfinder supplies the progression principle. Spell rank and action cost are separate in PF2e: Heal offers three action commitments at rank 1, while wizard progression unlocks higher spell ranks with level. These four Battlefield tiers describe troop capability and adapt that principle to army scale.

Playtest priorities

  • Threshold battles. Compare levels 5/6, 10/11, and 15/16. ReignMaker’s cost bands already step here; test whether simultaneous spell unlocks make those steps too strong. Level 11 adds six spells for every tradition, including its first spell in the weak tree.
  • Novice decisions. Check that apprentice turns offer useful combinations of magic, movement, and ordinary activities. Each tradition starts with three distinct domain choices. Compare Arcane’s damage and Morale pressure, Divine’s recovery and protection, Occult’s control and attack support, and Primal’s damage and recovery. Check that all four can contribute across offensive, defensive, and objective battles.
  • Formation effects. Storm’s fourth target, Terror’s three saves, and Gate’s paired transfer can change a short battle. Measure their practical opportunities and counterplay through spacing, pressure, and screening.
  • Recovery and protection. Renewal can restore several points of Health on one unit; Sanctuary can prevent Morale loss from damage on two. Check whether either prolongs battles beyond the intended dusk pacing.
  • Tradition balance. Every tradition has the same spell counts, spell-tier mix, and unlock timing, with sixteen spells and two masteries at the final tier. Each gains earlier or deeper access than every other tradition in some trees and pays for it in others. Compare practical effects at all four level bands: Primal combines early damage with recovery; Arcane combines damage with Morale pressure; Divine supports allies through recovery and protection; Occult combines control with attack support. Include concentration, automatic buffs, and the shared attack allowance in that comparison. Adjust effects or domain assignments if one combination dominates; apply the shared progression schedule to every tradition.
